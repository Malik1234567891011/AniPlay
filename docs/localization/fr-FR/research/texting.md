# French texting / chat conventions (2024–26)

**Evidence warning.** The hard France-specific corpora are old — Panckhurst's
**88milSMS** (2011) and Bernicot et al. (2009–10) document exactly the SMS era
you want to *avoid*. There is no public corpus of 2024–26 French Discord/Snap
writing. Sections below rest on those corpora as a *negative* baseline, on
2025–26 French lexicographic and press sources, and on judgment where flagged.

## The headline finding

**French 16–25s do not abbreviate heavily any more, and heavy abbreviation is
the single most reliable "cringe/vieux" tell available.** But the replacement is
not clean French — it is a different non-standard system.

**Why the old system died.** The 2008–12 style was driven by hard constraints:
alphanumeric keypads, T9 deliberately disabled, 160 characters, per-message
billing. Bernicot measured "textisme density" rising from **0.44 to 0.57** over
twelve months, messages averaging **7 words**: `c toi ki socupe d chien a Martin`,
`slt je vé pa au colèg é twa`. Every constraint is now gone. **The cost of
typing the full word dropped below the cost of inventing the abbreviation.**

**What survives** is a small **closed, lexicalised** set — `tkt, jsp, dsl, bcp,
mdr(r), ptdr, stp, pk, psk, jpp, dcp, mtn, nn` — typed as whole units.
**Productive abbreviation is dead**: nobody spontaneously shortens an arbitrary
word. A 2026 twenty-year-old writes "demain", not "2m1".

**What replaced it — four things:**
1. **Lowercase everything.** The strongest current marker, and a deliberate
   identity choice: capitals read as "sévère", "abrupt", school-coded.
2. **Clitic contraction without apostrophes, words otherwise in full**: `chui`,
   `jsuis`, `jpense`, `jte`, `cest`, `tas`, `ya`. This is *reduction of speech*,
   not *compression of text* — a completely different logic.
3. **Expressive lengthening replaces abbreviation** as the main affective
   device: `mdrrrr`, `ouiii`, `nonnn`, `graveee`. The one category that *grew*.
4. **New abbreviation is happening in English, not French.** A 2025 Gen Z
   lexicon lists GOAT, POV, IMO, IYKYK, NGL, GRWM, FOMO — **every entry is
   English-origin, none French.** The most useful structural fact here.

**Is heavy abbreviation cringe?** Yes — specifically the *rebus* kind. France
has a dedicated word, **kikoolol**, for late-2000s pre-teen internet writing.
`koi 2 9`, `a12c4`, sincere `jtm`, `XD` mark a character as a child, an
over-40 doing an impression, or a joke. **But the surviving core set is not
cringe — it is invisible. What is cringe is density.** One or two per message:
native. Four: caricature. **This is the single most important calibration rule.**

## Verdict table

**GREEN — ship freely**
`tkt` · `jsp` · `dsl` · `bcp` · `mdrr`/`mdrrr` · `ptdr` · `stp` · `pk` · `psk` ·
`jpp` · `dcp` · `mtn` · `nn`/`nan` · `ouais` · `chui`/`jsuis` · `bref` · `gg` ·
`rip` · `grv` · `en vrai` · `jrigole` (this is the French for "jk" — **"jk" is
never-French**)

**AMBER — current but marked; use for a character, not as house voice**
`wsh`/`wesh` · `frr`/`reuf` · `wallah` · `ptn` · `tmtc` · `bg` · `sah` · `cheh` ·
`aled` · `cc` (skews female, also the creepy-DM opener meme) · `slt` ·
`bjr` (reads ironic between peers) · `svp` (vouvoiement → adult/service) ·
`ez` (gamer) · `askip` (peaked 2016–20) · `vrmt` · English loans

**RED — dated or cringe**
`lol` (became a politeness filler then a boomer tell) · `xd` · `oklm` (reads
2015–18 hard) · `qqn` · `ct` · sincere `jtm` · `@+` (already flagged archaic in
2015) · `g` (=j'ai) · systematic accent-stripping · `!!!` · rebus digits
(`2m1`, `koi29`, `a12c4`, `bi1`) · `dtc`

**BLACK — do not ship**
`mgl` (ableist slur, genuinely used and genuinely offensive) · `tg` in any
non-affectionate context · Arabic-origin markers used to code a stereotype

**Missing from most lists but current:** `ptn`/`pt1` · `psk` · `jrigole` ·
`jtjure` · `wallah` · `grv` · `mtn` · `tmtc` · `osef` (fading) · `aled` ·
`cheh` · `en vrai` (**extremely high-frequency discourse opener**, the FR
"ngl/honestly") · `flemme`

## Orthographic markers

| Marker | Status |
|---|---|
| **Letter repetition** (`mdrrrr`, `ouiii`) | **The #1 current marker.** Length ≈ intensity. |
| **No capitals** | Near-obligatory in peer chat. |
| **No final period** | Near-obligatory. The line break does the work. |
| **Final period = cold/passive-aggressive** | **Real in France**, though the underlying study is American. A decade of French press has made it legible. **The cheapest characterisation device available.** |
| **Accents dropped** | Only partially — autocorrect restores most. **Do not strip systematically**; that reads 2009. |
| **Apostrophes dropped** | **Yes, and this is the good one.** `jsuis`, `cest`, `tas`, `ya`. |
| **"…"** | Reads older-generation or heavily passive-aggressive. Not neutral. |
| **`??` / `???`** | Current. `!!!` reads older/kikoo. |
| **Keyboard smash** | AZERTY: `azertyui`, `qsdfghj`. **ALL CAPS survives as pure shouting** even in an otherwise-lowercase style. |
| **Message splitting** | Strong marker. One thought = 2–4 short bubbles. |
| **No greeting/sign-off** | Total. Bernicot 2009 already had 85% with neither, rising to 91%. |

## Emoji

| Emoji | French meaning | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **💀** | "mort de rire" — **the pun lands harder in French than English** | **Default laughter marker.** |
| **😭** | Hilarity, not sadness | Current, safe |
| **😂** | **Ringard/boomer**, flagged since ~2021 | **RISKY** — use only to mark a character as older |
| **🤣** | Worse than 😂 | Avoid |
| **👍** | **Passive-aggressive, cold, condescending** to teens | Powerful characterisation tool |
| **🤡** | **Self-directed** in French usage — "j'ai été bête" | Safe |
| **🙂** | **Ironic contempt.** Never sincere | Current, sharp |
| **🫠 🥲 🖤 ✨ 🫣** | overwhelmed / bittersweet / dark humour / sarcasm / shame | Current |
| **❤️** | Still normal in France (a UK survey calling it "old" does not transfer) | Safe |
| **Emoji-only replies** | Fully normal — a single 💀 as a complete turn | **Use a lot.** Highest-density authenticity signal, costs zero translation |

## Rendering an in-fiction text message

**Precedents worth studying**, all French-*made* rather than localised:
*Enterre-moi, mon amour* (The Pixel Hunt / ARTE France, 2017) — an entire
narrative game built from WhatsApp-style bubbles, written in French first;
*A Normal Lost Phone* / *Another Lost Phone* (Accidental Queens).

**Spec:** render as bubbles, never as prose · split each turn into 2–4 short
bubbles · all lowercase · no final period (keep `?`) · drop apostrophes in
clitics · max 1–2 abbreviations per message from the green list · lengthen for
affect · keep accents mostly · **never** `2m1`, `XD`, sincere `jtm`, `@+`,
`slt cv ?` as a friend-opener.

**Good (close friend, 20):**
```
attends
tas vu ce quil a posté

jsuis morte 💀

nan mais srx
il assume meme pas
```

**Bad (2009 caricature):** `Slt cv? kestufé 2m1 ? jtm bcp bi1 sur a+ XD`

**Bad the other way (robot/teacher):** `Salut ! Est-ce que tu as vu ce qu'il a
publié ? Je trouve ça incroyable.`

**Use deviations as characterisation** — this is free and very legible:
full sentences with capitals and periods = the serious one, the older one, or
someone **furious** · replies only in 💀 and 😭 · uses 😂 = the uncool one ·
a bare `👍` = cold · **one long paragraph instead of bubbles** = emotionally
overloaded, or the confession scene.

**Give each character a texting fingerprint** — bubble count, emoji set, whether
they capitalise, `mdrr` vs 😭 vs nothing — and keep it consistent. French
readers in the target band read the fingerprint before they read the words.

## Register split

The switch is real, near-universal and consciously performed. A French
23-year-old, quoted: *"j'écris en minuscules avec mes amis, mais j'utilise des
majuscules au travail."* From the other side, Ipsos for Certificat Voltaire
found **73%** of decision-makers consider written-expression difficulties
disqualifying in hiring.

- **DM, close friend** — lowercase, no punctuation, 2–5 bubbles, `tu`, phonetic
  contractions, standalone 💀. No greeting or sign-off.
- **Group chat** — same base but **performative**: shorter, faster, more memes,
  reply-quoting, reaction spam. Sincerity migrates to DM.
- **Public comment** — still lowercase, but written for strangers: private
  shorthand disappears, formulaic community phrases appear (`en vrai`,
  `personne va parler de…`), often *more* full words than a DM.
- **To a teacher or boss** — **hard switch, near-total.** Capitals, accents,
  full punctuation, zero abbreviations, zero emoji, `vous`,
  `Bonjour Madame,` … `Cordialement`. Realistic failure mode for a young
  character: **overshooting** into
  `Veuillez agréer, Madame, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées` for a
  two-line email — a very authentic and very funny beat.
