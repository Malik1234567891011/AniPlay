# GENRE_GUIDES — the fourteen voices

Plotbreak's worlds do not share a narrator. In English they can get away with
sharing one, because English genre register is mostly carried by vocabulary. In
French it is carried by vocabulary **and** by tense, by `tu`/`vous`, by whether
`ne` survives, and by which of four words for the same object a character picks.
A single French narrator across fourteen worlds is the sound of a translation.

Each world below gets: the register, the vocabulary anchors, the starting
`addressMode` matrix, the traps, and one line pair.

Sources: [`research/nautical-pirate.md`](research/nautical-pirate.md),
[`research/espionage.md`](research/espionage.md),
[`research/horror-fantasy.md`](research/horror-fantasy.md),
[`research/texting.md`](research/texting.md).

## 0. The register ladder — the thing to internalise first

French genre writing runs on **choosing consciously between synonyms that are
not synonyms**. The nautical research produced the cleanest example, and the
pattern generalises to every world:

| Word for "ship" | Register | Who says it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bateau` | everyday, civilian | anyone, slightly landlubber |
| **`navire`** | standard written | **the narrator's default** |
| `bâtiment` | naval-professional | Marine Nationale officers only |
| `vaisseau` | historical, literary | age of sail, emphatic |
| `le rafiot` | affectionate-pejorative | the crew about their own ship |

The same ladder exists for police (`flic` / `policier` / `fonctionnaire de
police`), for intelligence (`indic` / `informateur` / `source`), for operations
(`opé` / `opération` / `dispositif`), for meetings (`rencard` / `rendez-vous` /
`rencontre`). **Which rung a character stands on is characterisation, and a
translated text stands on the same rung all the time.**

## 1. Blackwake — pirates, age of sail

**Register.** One Piece base, Varlet garnish: plain modern job nouns, authentic
ship parts and commands. Narration `navire`; pirate dialogue `navire`/`bateau`;
the Crown's officers `bâtiment`/`vaisseau`; the crew's own ship `le rafiot`.

**Anchors.** `la barre` (the helm — **not** `le gouvernail`, which is the rudder
blade) · `le gaillard d'arrière` (quarterdeck — **not** `la dunette`, which is a
deck higher) · `les fers` / `mettre aux fers` (the brig — `le brick` is a
two-masted ship and the false friend is total) · `appareiller` (set sail —
**never** `mettre les voiles`, which is modern slang for doing a runner) ·
`la hune` (crow's nest — `le nid-de-pie` is a calque) · `la vigie` (lookout,
**feminine even for a man**) · `le coq` (cook) · `le bosco` (bosun) ·
`le gabier` (topman) · `le second` (first mate) · `le pavillon`, never
`le drapeau` · `à l'abordage !` · `le branle-bas de combat` ·
`la coupée` (the boarding gangway — `la passerelle` is a modern ship's bridge).

**The crew.** Default word for the pirates: **`les flibustiers`** (Varlet's
choice for Flint's men). `corsaire` is not a synonym — corsairs hold a
`lettre de marque`, are legal, and are **national heroes in France**.
`boucanier` is not a synonym either. `les gentilshommes de fortune` is
period-perfect and ironic. `Jolly Roger` stays English, as it does in Varlet.

**The `quartier-maître` landmine.** Historiographically correct for the pirate
role and used by Varlet for Long John Silver — but in the modern French Navy it
is the **lowest rank above ordinary seaman**. Use it, and establish the role
in-fiction the first time: `le quartier-maître, second après le capitaine et élu
par l'équipage`. Where seniority must be unambiguous, `le second`.

**Traps.** « Mille sabords ! » is 100 % Capitaine Haddock — a French player
hears Tintin. Same for « Tonnerre de Brest ! ». Signature oath instead:
**« Mille tonnerres ! »** (attested in Varlet). Never `Sacrebleu`. Never
`marcher sur la planche` — French has no idiom; use « À la planche ! ».
Avoid `marronnage` for marooning (it means the escape of enslaved people); use
`le marron` for the man and `abandonner sur une île déserte` for the act.
A sailor never says `corde`; a landlubber saying it and being corrected is a
free authenticity beat.

**addressMode.** Captain Veyra Sol → **vous** to the player, and it is the whole
relationship. Nessa Vale, Rook Arden, Mako Renn → **tu** (crewmates). Tolla
Corrow (shipwright, older, a business relationship) → **vous** until the debt is
settled. Harrow Bell → **vous**, always, from both sides — that is the point of
him.

```
❌  Soudain, le capitaine cria : « Mille sabords ! Hissez les voiles ! »
✅  Veyra ne hausse pas la voix. « Paré à virer ? »
    Rook, quelque part au-dessus de toi : « Paré ! »
```

## 2. Last Five — high-school basketball, Japan

**Register.** Modern, spoken, short. Present tense on the court. This is the
world where French youth slang is not decoration but the medium.

**Anchors.** `la raquette` (the paint) · `un lancer franc` · `une passe
décisive` (`une passe déc'`) · `une perte de balle` (turnover) · `un contre`
(block) · `un écran` (screen) · `une contre-attaque` (fast break) ·
`la prolongation` (overtime) · `le meneur` / `l'arrière` / `l'ailier` /
`l'ailier fort` / `le pivot` · `un temps mort` · `il en a planté 30` ·
`le coach` in speech, `l'entraîneur` in narration.

**Traps.** ① **Do not domesticate the setting.** Kosei is a Japanese school. No
`UNSS`, no `seconde/première/terminale`, no `le bac`. `le lycée`, `le club`,
`le tournoi national`. ② The world's figurative-violence register —
`je l'explose`, `je le fume`, `il s'est fait démonter`, `je l'ai défoncé` — is
**ordinary basketball French and must not resolve as assault.** See
[`PLAYER_GRAMMAR.md` §2.6](PLAYER_GRAMMAR.md). ③ The premise is literally
*"beat the five who left"*; a French verb lexicon has to survive a world made of
hitting, taking and going at people.

**addressMode.** Every teammate → **tu**, immediately, including the ones who
hate you. Ena Torakawa (coach) → **vous** to the player; the player's `fromPlayer`
→ **vous**. Rivals from other schools → **tu**, and the `tu` is aggressive.

```
❌  Tu te diriges vers le panier et marques un point spectaculaire.
✅  Tu pars en un-contre-un. Jun te suit d'un demi-pas. Tu montes.
    Le ballon touche la planche et rentre.
```

## 3. Window Seven — espionage, stakeout *(bible; not yet built)*

**Register.** Restraint, not adrenaline. *Le Bureau des Légendes*, not Bond:
*« sérieux et rigueur »*, no *« lexique rocambolesque à la Q »*. Gadget-porn and
wisecracks read as American. Short verbless sentences, present tense for action,
passé composé rather than passé simple.

**The finding that changes the world.** In official French usage **`un agent` is
the recruited source, not the officer.** « Tu es notre meilleur agent » reads as
*"you are our best informant"*. The insider words for the player are
**`officier traitant`** (`l'OT`, `le traitant`), `officier de renseignement`,
`clandestin`. **Exploit the split:** civilians and in-game news say `agent`; the
service says `OT`. That contrast alone carries the authenticity.

**Anchors, used flatly and never glossed.** `une planque` (stakeout *and* safe
house — there is no other word; « une maison sûre » is a calque) ·
`une filature` / `filer` / `semer` · `les écoutes` (plural) ·
`la sonorisation` (bugging a room) · `une BLM` (boîte aux lettres morte) ·
`relever une BLM` · `un coupe-circuit` · `le cloisonnement` ·
**« Vous n'avez pas le besoin d'en connaître. »** · `une taupe` (**feminine even
for a man** — « La taupe, c'est lui. ») · `une couverture` / `griller` /
`brûlé` / `grillé` / `cramé` · `sous légende` · `l'exfiltration` ·
`un dispositif` · `des remontées` · `un HC` (honorable correspondant, unpaid,
patriotic — a doctrinally load-bearing and uniquely French distinction from a
paid `agent`) · `l'entrave` · `le service Action` · `une opé`.

**No French word exists for:** *intel*, *asset*, *tradecraft*, *safe house*,
*burn notice*, *handler*, *chatter*, *black site*. **Do not calque any of them.**
Use `le renseignement` / `une note`; `un relais`, `des moyens`; **`le métier`**
(« Il connaît le métier. »); `une planque`; « il est brûlé, le service ne le
reconnaît plus »; « ça parle beaucoup »; `une prison secrète`. **Never
`le manipulateur` for handler** — that means a manipulative creep.

**Service nicknames are the best register lever available.** `la Piscine` is
what **outsiders** say; an officer using it is a tell that they are not really
inside. Insiders say `la Boîte`, `la Crèmerie`, `Mortier`.

**addressMode.** Mara Ellison ↔ player → **vous**, and the seven nights are the
arc. Director Elias Halden → **vous**, permanently, both ways. Dr Selene Voss →
**vous**, then a `tu` that is a *manipulation move*, not intimacy. « Glass » →
whatever destabilises. **A handler switching to `tu` is tradecraft.** Write it
that way and a French player will notice the product knows something.

```
❌  « Oh mon Dieu, nous avons perdu la cible ! » cria-t-elle.
✅  « Rupture de filature. »  Mara repose les jumelles.  « On décroche. »
```

## 4. Hush House — supernatural horror, romance *(bible; not yet built)*

**Register.** **Le fantastique**, not "horror": Todorov's *« hésitation entre le
surnaturel et le naturel »*, realism as the platform. Present tense, first
person for anything diary-shaped, concrete verb chains.

**Anchors.** Points de suspension as the tension unit (`serre… serre…`). Precise
sound nouns — `un craquement`, `un grincement`, `un raclement`, `un cliquetis` —
never spelled onomatopoeia. Banal precision as the anchor: `vers deux heures
treize`, `deux tours de clef`. Non-visual senses. Narrator self-interrogation:
`tu as peur… de quoi ?`

**Traps.** `grand-guignolesque` is a real French pejorative and piling on
violence lands directly in it. The cliché blocklist is a hard fail:
`un frisson parcourut son échine` · `horreur indicible` · `paralysé d'effroi` ·
`silence pesant` · `les ténèbres t'enveloppent` · `sueur froide` ·
`son cœur battait la chamade`. And the Papy lesson: **do not add a metaphysics
the source lacks.** No `le Ciel`, no `l'Enfer`, no `damnée` unless the world
authored a religion.

**addressMode.** Ayame Kurose → **vous**, then a `tu` that is the romance beat,
and — if the player pushes into the wrong door — a `TU → VOUS` reversion that is
the coldest thing this world can do. Mrs. Elara Vale (landlady, older) →
**vous** both ways. Tomas Reed, Nia Bell (tenants, peers) → **tu**. « Mika » →
**tu**, from the first word, and that is wrong in a way the player should feel.

```
❌  Une horreur indicible t'envahit tandis que les ténèbres s'épaississent.
✅  Tu montes vers deux heures. Tu donnes deux tours de clef.
    À 2 h 13, quelqu'un frappe.  Tu comptes… un… deux…
```

## 5. Good Morning, Husband — domestic romance, comedy *(bible; not yet built)*

**Register.** Warm, ordinary, present tense, very short beats. The hardest
register in French to get right, because *nice* French written by a translator
comes out saccharine.

**Anchors.** Domestic specificity: `le café qui refroidit`, `la porte du frigo`,
`ta place à table`, `le bruit de la douche`. Terms of endearment are a minefield
of dubbing — `mon chéri` / `ma chérie` are fine, `bébé` is current among young
couples, `mon amour` is real but heavier than English "love", and **`mon
cœur`** is the warmest everyday one. `mon ange` reads maternal. `poussin`,
`mon lapin`, `ma puce` are real and mark a specific kind of couple.

**Traps.** `Tu me complètes.` and `Je suis tellement chanceux de t'avoir.` are
calques. French intimacy is understated and physical: `Je suis mieux quand t'es
là.` Do not translate American domestic comedy rhythm; French comedy runs on
deadpan and on the thing left unsaid.

**addressMode.** Hana Mori ↔ player → **tu**, from the first line, because they
are married and there is no other possibility. **That is a fact the French
version knows and the English version cannot express**, and the memory-loss
premise makes it a joke: the player wakes up and is *already* being tutoyé by
someone they do not remember. Kenji Mori (father-in-law) → **vous** to the
player, **tu** to Hana. Emi Takeda → **tu**.

```
❌  « Bonjour, mon amour ! Es-tu prêt pour cette merveilleuse journée ? »
✅  « T'as encore laissé le lait dehors. »  Elle t'embrasse quand même.
```

## 6. Primal Crown — prehistoric factions, beast riding *(bible; not yet built)*

**Register.** Plain, physical, present tense. **Not** archaic. This is where the
Sola lesson bites hardest: a prehistoric setting is the maximum temptation to
reach for `derechef` and `jouvenceau`, and it is the maximum penalty.

**Anchors.** Flavour in **nouns and names, never in syntax**. Coin faction names
from real French morphology and hyphenate compounds like real French toponymy —
that is the single strongest signal of natively-conceived French. Beast
vocabulary should be concrete and physical: `la monture`, `le harnais`,
`les fanons`, `l'échine`, `le garrot`, `la horde`, `la transhumance` (a real
French word for seasonal migration and a gift for this world).

**Traps.** No Earth references inside a secondary world (`froid polaire` is the
canonical error). No Title Case: `la horde de Cendre`, not `La Horde De Cendre`.
Peoples capitalise as nouns and lowercase as adjectives: `les Emberclaw`, but
`un cavalier emberclaw`. Freeze every invented noun's gender **before** any
prose is written — see [`TERMINOLOGY.md`](TERMINOLOGY.md).

**addressMode.** Within a tribe → **tu**. Across tribes → **vous**, and the first
`tu` across a faction line is a diplomatic event. To a beast → **tu**, always.
Kaia Thorn → **tu**. Torren Vale → **vous** until earned.

## 7. The Ninth Archive — magic academy, investigation

**Register.** Institutional French against a nineteen-year-old. The comedy and
the menace both come from that gap.

**Anchors.** `le règlement`, `le dossier`, `la procédure`, `l'inscription`,
`le registre`, `un recours`, `convoquer`, `le conseil de discipline`. French
institutional vocabulary is *specific* and every French person has been on the
wrong side of it, which makes it land harder than any invented magic-school
jargon. Ward → `le scellé` or `la protection`; sigil → `le sigle` is a false
friend (it means an acronym) — use **`le sceau`** or `le signe`.

**addressMode.** Warden Ysolde Farrow → **vous** to the player, and never moves.
Mira Senn, Kael Ostrand, Bram Ketch → **tu**. **The moment Farrow says `tu` to
you is the moment you are in real trouble**, and it is a scene worth authoring.

## 8. The Understudy — theatre, rivalry

**Register.** Theatre French is a real closed register and using it correctly is
free authenticity: `la générale` (dress rehearsal) · `le filage` · `la doublure`
(understudy — the title itself) · `les italiennes` · `le raccord` ·
`le plateau` · `la coulisse` · `le lointain` / `la face` · `le régisseur` ·
`la couturière` · `le trac` · `« Merde ! »` — **the French theatre good-luck
wish, which must never be translated as an insult.** That one line is worth the
whole research pass.

**addressMode.** Marta Voss (director) → **vous**, and the company `vous` her
back. Talia Renn → **vous** at first (professional rivals), collapsing to a `tu`
that is *worse*. Oswin Deare → **tu** immediately, because he has no boundaries.

## 9. The Salt Road — desert survival, lethal

**Register.** Stripped. The shortest sentences in the catalogue. Arithmetic as
prose: `neuf jours d'eau, onze jours de marche`. Present tense. Almost no
adjectives — this world's beauty is in the numbers.

**Anchors.** `la gourde`, `l'outre`, `le sel`, `la caravane`, `l'étape`,
`le relais`, `rationner`, `la réverbération`, `l'ombre portée`. Note
`la galère` — `c'est la galère` is extremely common modern French and would be
wrong here for exactly one reason: register clash with a world with no jokes in
it.

**addressMode.** Ferrow → **vous**, professional, a paid escort. Oren → **tu**.
Sabe → **vous**, then never changes, and the not-changing is the character.

## 10. The Tidewall — high fantasy, class RPG, military

**Register.** Military French, modern in syntax, medieval in nouns. Lauzon, not
Sola. `la garde`, `la relève`, `le poste`, `l'ordre`, `la consigne`,
`rompez`, `à vos rangs`. Rank and address are the whole texture.

**Traps.** Class names are invented nouns and need frozen gender. Do **not**
render `long sword` as `flamberge`. Do **not** invert (`une couronne d'acier il
portait`).

**addressMode.** Drillmaster Odalys Verne → **tu** to recruits (asymmetric, and
the asymmetry is the power); **vous** from them. Commander Sabel Ansett →
**vous** both ways. Bec Tarrow, Hollis Ferrant → **tu**. A drillmaster who
suddenly *vouvoies* a recruit is being sarcastic or is about to say something
serious — a free, entirely French beat.

## 11. The Unbound — martial arts, anime, coming of age

**Register.** The most anime-adjacent world, and therefore the one where the
temptation to explain is highest. **Do not explain.** France is one of the
world's largest manga markets; a gloss on what a school, a form or a master is
reads as an American product talking to Americans.

**Anchors.** `l'école` (the school as a lineage), `la forme`, `le maître`,
`le disciple`, `la voie`, `le souffle`, `l'enchaînement`, `la garde`,
`la posture`. French martial-arts vocabulary is settled and precise because judo
and karate are mass sports in France — use it and it will sound native.

**Traps.** Invented technique names are the gender minefield of this catalogue —
`Ember Palm`, `Turning Tide`, `Gale Step`, `The Unnamed Form`. Every one needs a
frozen French gender before a line of prose exists.

**addressMode.** Reader Auber Kell → **vous**. Renna Vosk, Tam Ashgrove → **tu**.
Sera Ilm → **vous**, deliberately, from someone the same age — which in French
is a whole characterisation on its own.

## 12. Nine Weeks — romance, slice of life, summer

**Register.** The most spoken French in the catalogue. `ne` dropped everywhere,
`on` for `nous` everywhere, `t'es` and `t'as` everywhere. Texting is a first-
class surface here — see [`research/texting.md`](research/texting.md) and
render it as bubbles, never as prose.

**The tu/vous arc.** This world can spend nine weeks on it and should. `vous` at
the staff cabins in week one is *funny* between two twenty-year-olds — it is
someone being deliberately distant. The slip into `tu` in week four is a beat
one of them notices out loud. The return to `vous` after the week-seven argument
is a wound with no English equivalent at all.

**addressMode.** Juno Vale → the arc. Teo Sandoval, Nadia Okonkwo → **tu**.
Cass Reyner → **vous**, and refuses to stop, which is the joke and then is not.

## 13. Red Moon Brigade — monster hunt, body horror

**Register.** Military plus clinical. Two vocabularies deliberately colliding:
`le rapport`, `l'ordre permanent`, `la relève` against `le prélèvement`,
`la culture`, `l'échantillon`, `la dégradation`, `le sujet`. The horror is that
the second set is being applied to the player.

**Traps.** Body horror is where `grand-guignolesque` is one adjective away at
all times. Name what is happening in plain clinical French and stop. `Dr Elian
Voss` — note `Dr` takes **no period** in French (`Dr`, `Mme`, `Mlle`, `M.` is
the only one that does).

**addressMode.** Commander Serah Vale → **vous**. Captain Lyra Venn → **vous** to
the player, **tu** to Ren Calder. Dr Elian Voss → **vous**, clinically, and it is
worse than shouting. Wick → **tu**, always, to everyone, including the
commander.

## 14. Seven Days to Midnight — time loop, mystery, slice of life

**Register.** Ordinary, warm, precise — and then the same sentences again. The
time loop is a **French typography gift**: `Jour 3 · 16:15` repeating exactly,
day parts repeating exactly, a `mardi` that keeps coming back. Lowercase
weekdays, 24-hour clock, and the repetition does the work.

**Traps.** The loop means the same beat is written many times. This is where the
French will show any wobble: an invented noun that changes gender between loops,
a character who switches to `tu` on Tuesday and back on Wednesday, a `1 janvier`
that should be `1er janvier`. **This world is the T/V and glossary regression
test.** Play it in French before shipping anything.

**addressMode.** Mina Arclight → **vous**, and the player can spend loops earning
`tu` and then lose it on reset — which is the most cruelly elegant use of this
mechanic available anywhere in the catalogue, and worth authoring explicitly.
Detective Sera Wynn → **vous**. Theo Marr → **tu**. Councillor Ada Harrow →
**vous**, permanently.

## The cross-world rule

**Register is per world, per character, per scene — never per product.** If
Blackwake's narrator and Nine Weeks' narrator sound the same in French, the
French is translated, however good each individual sentence is.
