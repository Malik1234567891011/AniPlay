# Three new launch worlds — requirements

**Status: built.** The Tidewall (`tidewall.ts`), The Unbound
(`unbound.ts`) and Nine Weeks (`nine-weeks.ts`) are in `LAUNCH_CATALOG` and
pass the §43.1 content bar and the clarity checks. This file stays as the
source of the requirements they were built against.

Recorded 2026-09-09 from a direct product brief. These are **additions**. The
three existing worlds (The Ninth Archive, The Understudy, The Salt Road) are not
to be replaced, reworded, or removed, and their art is locked.

## Rules that apply to all three

- **Original IP.** They may draw on familiar anime and RPG fantasies, but no
  copyrighted characters, clans, locations, or terminology uniquely associated
  with another franchise, and no retelling of an existing plot.
- **Narrative clarity, unchanged.** Mystery is not knowing *why*; confusion is
  not knowing *what*. The premise must be immediately understandable on first
  read. Concrete first, lore later. Depth is welcome; vagueness and artificial
  complication are not. These pass `checkNarrativeClarity` like every other
  world.
- Same authored quality bar as the existing three (§43.1): a one-line hook, a
  premise in paragraphs, a first real decision inside one turn, a cast with
  independent motives, one strong deterministic system, five seeded promises.
- Compatible with freeform "say or do anything" input. Choices and state must
  actually change what happens — no world where one route is real and everything
  else is scenery.
- Deterministic engine, AI presentation. Nothing resolves into open-ended
  chatbot roleplay.

## 1. Class-born fantasy RPG

Everyone in this world develops into one of five combat classes. The class must
change how the player fights, progresses, explores, and is treated — not a
character-creation menu in front of one shared story.

| Class | Fantasy | Signature progression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warrior | Raw power, armour, charging in | **Mounts** that escalate: horse → war beast → wyvern/dragon tier |
| Rogue | Fast, agile, opportunistic; daggers, twin blades, chained weapons | **Bonded companions** that fight beside you: wolf/hawk/big cat → tiger, shadow beast → legendary |
| Archer | Long-range precision and mobility; bow types, trick arrows, tracking, high ground | Needs its **own** escalation equal to mounts/companions |
| Sorcerer | Schools and elements; destruction, defence, illusion, summoning, utility | Needs its **own** escalation |
| Healer | **Must not be boring.** Restoration, protection, buffs, cleansing, late resurrection-adjacent power | Must support a genuinely aggressive battle-healer build |

Class must influence: abilities, combat, progression, equipment, quests and
opportunities, how NPCs read you, class rivalries, guilds and factions, major
decisions, which parts of the world open, and the long-term power fantasy. The
ceiling should feel absurd in the best way.

Needs an accessible central plot giving a reason to adventure beyond levelling.

## 2. Original ninja / internal-energy world

The appeal is **extreme build freedom**: "I am growing into my own completely
unique fighter, and my build could end up wildly different from someone else's."

Customisable over time: energy affinity, elemental affinities, fighting style,
techniques, specialisation (hand-to-hand / weapons / ranged / illusion), rare
inherited abilities, awakened visual powers, summons, forbidden techniques,
advanced states, and signature techniques that become *theirs*.

Not everything is picked from a menu at the start. Abilities should also awaken
from events, be taught by mentors, come out of relationships, depend on
behaviour, follow from lineage, fall out of major choices, or be rare finds.

Original world, factions, terminology, visual powers, politics and mythology.
Explicitly **not** Naruto: no its characters, villages, clans, dojutsu, or plot.

Strong central story: rival factions, friendships, mentors, enemies, missions,
escalating threat.

## 3. Romance and heartbreak drama

Primarily about relationships, not fighting. Capable of genuine romance **and**
genuine heartbreak. Not "pick which attractive person you date".

Must support: attraction, friendship, jealousy, misunderstanding, loyalty,
betrayal, reconciliation, unrequited feeling, exes, competing relationships,
hard choices, people changing over time, relationships that genuinely fail,
consequences for treating people badly, and falling for someone unexpected.

NPCs have their own feelings, boundaries, and relationships **with each other**.
They do not exist to fall for the player. Someone must be able to reject the
player, lose feelings, choose someone else, forgive, refuse to forgive, get
jealous, betray, get hurt, and reconnect much later. That uncertainty is what
makes a successful romance mean anything.

Setting should naturally produce closeness and friction — school, a shared
summer, young adults living together, a creative industry, a team. Humour and
happy moments belong in it; it is not unrelenting misery.

Fantasy: "I am living my own addictive romance anime, and I genuinely do not
know who I will end up with or whether it works out."
