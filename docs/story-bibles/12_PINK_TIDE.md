# PINK TIDE

**Plotbreak official-world concept**  
**Genre:** beach mystery / summer romance / resort thriller / feel-good slice of life / ensemble secrets / light adventure  
**Setting:** **Aster Cove**, an adults-only island resort off the fictional subtropical coast of southern Japan  
**Default player:** adult, 21–29  
**Core fantasy:** You arrive at paradise for one week. The water is absurdly blue, the nightlife is effortless, and the gorgeous pink-haired resort host who keeps flirting with you seems determined to make your vacation unforgettable. Then she tells you a guest has been missing for three days—and management insists he checked out this morning.

**One-line hook:** **You came to paradise for a week. The hottest girl at the pool says someone disappeared.**

**Secondary hook:** **At Aster Cove, everybody came to escape something. One of them may have made sure somebody else never left.**

**Player promise:** Swim, party, flirt, fall in love, investigate, accuse the wrong person, save someone, protect a secret, expose a corporation, leave on day two, stay for months, or spend the whole week by the pool while the mystery continues without you.

---

# 1. DESIGN NORTH STAR

PINK TIDE lives or dies on one tonal trick:

> **The resort has to remain genuinely desirable even while something is wrong.**

The player should want to be here.

Sunlight on the pool. Cold drinks. Music carrying over the water. Linen shirts. Night swims. Paddleboards. Room-service breakfast. Beach volleyball. A ridiculous suite. The feeling that nobody expects anything from you until checkout.

Then underneath that:
- a guest who supposedly left but did not;
- a bag where it should not be;
- a staff member who lies for a reason that initially looks harmless;
- a restricted cove;
- a beautiful place whose history is less clean than its brochure.

The ideal feeling is:

> **“I want another day here even though I am increasingly sure something happened.”**

The romance and mystery must not cannibalize each other. The best scenes should sometimes contain both:
- Sora flirting with you while secretly watching a manager across the pool;
- investigating under a cabana while a waiter keeps delivering drinks;
- kissing in glowing water and noticing a light moving along a forbidden cliff path;
- eating pancakes the morning after a terrifying night because the resort breakfast is still excellent.

The darkness works because paradise remains beautiful.

# 2. STORY DNA — WHAT TO LEARN FROM RESORT MYSTERIES

The world is original, but the structure borrows useful lessons from resort and closed-circle mysteries.

## A. A resort is a pressure cooker disguised as freedom

A resort temporarily mixes:
- guests;
- staff;
- couples;
- strangers;
- rich people;
- workers;
- people celebrating;
- people hiding.

Everyone shares the same restaurants, pool, bars, hallways and ferries.

Vacation creates instant intimacy because ordinary life has been suspended.

## B. Almost everybody has a secret, but not every secret is the mystery

This is critical.

One guest is hiding debt.
Another is having an affair.
Another is lying about why they came.
Another is breaking staff rules.
One is genuinely involved in the disappearance.

If every lie points to the central crime, the mystery feels mechanical.

## C. Bright settings intensify danger

A shadowy clue in a basement is ordinary thriller language.

A bloodstain beneath a white cabana at 9:00 a.m. while someone twenty feet away orders mango pancakes is disturbing precisely because the world is still sunny.

## D. Suspects must remain characters after suspicion moves away

Once Luka is proven innocent, he should still be:
- Sora’s messy ex-fling;
- a dive instructor;
- funny;
- useful;
- someone with a life.

A mystery ensemble is not a row of disposable clue containers.

## E. The main girl cannot merely be a sexy clue dispenser

Sora is deliberately visually attention-grabbing. That is part of the product fantasy.

She still needs:
- her own career;
- her own dream;
- a reason she notices the mystery;
- a lie of her own;
- an emotional weakness;
- choices that happen without the player.

The player should still want to spend fifty turns with her after the case is solved.

# 3. TITLE — PINK TIDE

Once or twice each year, harmless bioluminescent microorganisms create a faint rose-magenta glow in the shallows around Aster Cove.

Locals call it:

## **THE PINK TIDE**

At night, swimmers leave pink light behind them.

The resort markets it heavily.

It works on three levels:
- the pink-haired heroine;
- the dreamy pink summer aesthetic;
- beautiful water hiding something beneath it.

The Pink Tide is **not supernatural** by default.

# 6. OPENING

**2:17 p.m.**

The stone around the infinity pool is hot enough to punish bare feet.

The player has been on the island maybe forty minutes.

They have not unpacked.

They are staring at the water, the bar, or their phone.

Then somebody splashes them.

Deliberately.

A woman in the shallow end pushes wet pink hair over one shoulder and looks completely unrepentant.

> “You’ve been here forty minutes and you still look like you’re at an airport.”

She looks them over.

> “That’s unacceptable.”

This is Sora.

Do not open with the disappearance.

Let paradise arrive first.

# 22. THE PRIVATE TRUTH — ADRIAN’S INVESTIGATION

Adrian came to Aster Cove investigating an old environmental cover-up connected to the resort’s eastern expansion zone.

Eight years earlier, during construction, the parent company temporarily stored industrial solvent barrels inside old sea caves at east cove after a mainland logistics emergency.

Most were removed.

At least two ruptured.

A contracted dive worker later became seriously ill.

The matter was suppressed through:
- settlement;
- incomplete cleanup report;
- aggressive legal confidentiality;
- favorable private testing.

Now the resort’s parent company wants to expand the marina into the same cove.

Adrian received leaked documents suggesting the cleanup was incomplete.

The contamination is **localized**.

The entire resort/ocean is not poisonous.

This distinction is crucial to preserving the paradise fantasy.

# 25. WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED

On the second night before the player arrives, Adrian sneaks toward east cove with a camera.

Assistant manager Eli Mercer catches him near a restricted service path.

Eli confronts him over:
- trespassing;
- confidential documents;
- filming.

They struggle over the camera.

Adrian goes over a low cliff/service ledge.

Eli did not arrive intending to kill him.

Whether it was:
- shove;
- grab;
- accidental loss of footing

He cannot immediately find a body.

He panics.

Then he makes the decision that turns an accident/assault into a thriller:

> **He covers it up.**

# 26. ELI’S COVER-UP

Eli:

- removes a segment of relevant security footage.

He intends to dispose of the bag later.

He does not know whether Adrian survived.

Every hour he waits makes the moral situation worse.

# 123. FINAL CREATIVE BAR

### Run 1 — Summer Romance
Player helps a little, lets police do most investigation, spends the week swimming/partying and continues dating Sora after vacation.

### Run 2 — Detective
Player obsesses over the timeline, catches the fake checkout, finds Adrian early and exposes Eli plus the corporate environmental history.

### Run 3 — Paradise
Player refuses investigation completely, has a genuinely fantastic week, becomes friends with staff and leaves refreshed. Weeks later Sora texts that something insane happened after they left.

### Run 4 — Corporate
Player takes a settlement, forces a private cleanup and keeps the worst scandal contained. Sora sees compromise; Nami sees cowardice.

### Run 5 — Wrong Person
Player publicly accuses Luka. He loses his job. The truth later clears him. Solving the case does not undo what the player did.

### Run 6 — Eli Confesses
Player realizes Eli is panicking rather than hunting people, corners him emotionally instead of physically, and gets Adrian’s approximate fall location in time.

### Run 7 — Too Late
Player treats it as vacation gossip for too long. Adrian dies. Paradise remains beautiful, which makes the consequence worse.

### Run 8 — Sora’s Future
Mystery resolves early. The real remaining drama is whether Sora takes a promotion or finally opens Tide & Salt.

### Run 9 — Just Summer
Player and Sora have an intense joyful week. At the ferry they admit neither wants long distance. They kiss goodbye and mean it.

### Run 10 — Stay
Player arrived for seven nights. Three months later they are helping Sora stack paddleboards at sunset and wondering when exactly the vacation ended.

All ten are unmistakably **PINK TIDE**.

Because its identity is not:

> **a murder mystery that happens to have a pool.**

It is:

> **a vacation you genuinely do not want to end, with one beautiful, flirtatious person pulling you deeper into a secret that becomes harder to ignore the more at home you feel there.**
