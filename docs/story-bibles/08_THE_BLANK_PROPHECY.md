# THE BLANK PROPHECY

**Plotbreak official-world concept**  
**Genre:** modern Greek mythology / anime adventure / road-trip fantasy / mystery / comedy / romance / gods and monsters  
**Setting:** present-day Greece, beginning in Athens and expanding through Delphi, the islands, Crete, the Peloponnese, the Underworld and Olympus  
**Core fantasy:** Every hero has a prophecy. Yours comes back completely blank. The gods cannot see what you will do, monsters begin hunting you, and a former hero who wants to cut humanity free from Fate believes you are the proof his revolution can work.

**One-line hook:** **Every hero gets a prophecy. Yours came back blank.**

**Secondary hook:** **The gods don’t know what you’re going to do. That terrifies them.**

---

# 1. DESIGN NORTH STAR

THE BLANK PROPHECY should feel like a funny, fast, emotionally grounded modern Greek-myth adventure with the visual excitement of anime.

The fantasy is not:

> memorize forty Greek names, pick a god-parent, collect quest markers.

It is:

> **Greek myth is real. I can walk through modern Athens and end up bargaining with Hermes in a closed metro station, fighting a monster on a ferry, arguing with Artemis over whether she gets to tell me what kind of hero I am, crossing into the Underworld while still alive, and becoming the one person in existence whose next move the gods genuinely cannot predict.**

The player should be able to:
- accept divine help;
- reject the gods;
- earn powers;
- invent their own powers;
- become a hero;
- become a menace;
- side with the antagonist;
- date someone;
- hate everyone;
- become friends with monsters;
- kill mythic characters;
- die;
- enter the Underworld;
- refuse the central quest;
- leave Greece;
- create a new cult/title;
- become divine;
- stay completely mortal;
- rewrite the relationship between gods and humans.

**Author the mythic world deeply. Never author the player’s route deeply.**

The story must work for someone who knows nothing about Greek mythology.

A mythology nerd should constantly notice deeper references.

A normal player should never feel like they are studying for a test.

---

# 2. RESEARCH DNA — WHAT TO LEARN WITHOUT COPYING

This story is original.

Do not reproduce:
- Percy Jackson characters;
- Camp Half-Blood;
- demigod-parent sorting;
- cabins;
- proprietary magic items;
- Hades character designs/dialogue;
- Saint Seiya armor;
- Fate-series summoning systems;
- specific anime combat systems;
- proprietary terminology.

Instead, borrow high-level storytelling lessons.

---

## A. Rick Riordan / modern myth adventure — mythology becomes fun when it collides with ordinary life

The useful lesson is not “teenagers are children of gods.”

It is:
- ancient myth can exist beside normal modern life;
- humor keeps exposition light;
- protagonists should meet gods as personalities, not textbook entries;
- monsters become memorable when they appear in places we understand;
- mystery should keep pages turning;
- young readers do not need to be written down to.

THE BLANK PROPHECY should have:
- buses;
- ferries;
- cheap food;
- phones;
- group chats;
- awkward hotels;
- bad weather;
- arguments over money;
- gods who understand modern life unevenly;
- mythological danger interrupting ordinary situations.

The writing can be clever and funny without turning every character into a joke machine.

---

## B. Hades — gods should feel like a family full of contradictions

A useful adaptation principle from modern Greek-myth games is:

Greek gods become memorable when they are:
- powerful;
- flawed;
- related;
- petty;
- loving;
- political;
- contradictory.

Hades is especially useful as evidence that a modern adaptation can assume no prior mythology knowledge while still rewarding people who know the sources.

THE BLANK PROPHECY should do the same.

Do not present:
**ZEUS — GOD OF SKY — POWER 9000**

Instead:
let the player learn what gods are through what they want and how they behave.

---

## C. Greek epithets — one god can have many faces

Historical Greek worship used many epithets/titles associated with a deity’s function, place or character.

Examples include:
- Artemis **Agrotera**, the Huntress;
- Hermes **Dolios**, associated with wiles/craft;
- Hermes **Agoraios**, of the marketplace;
- Athena **Polias**, protector of the city;
- Athena **Areia**, a warlike aspect;
- Apollo **Paian**, healer;
- Apollo **Moiragetes**, associated with guiding fate;
- Aphrodite **Areia**, a warlike aspect.

This gives us a genuinely Greek foundation for an anime power system.

Instead of:

**your father is Apollo, therefore you get sun powers**

the system becomes:

> **A god may give a mortal one of their Names — an Epithet — when that mortal begins embodying that aspect of the god.**

The same deity can produce radically different powers.

This is more flexible and more interesting for Plotbreak.

---

## D. The Moirai / Fates — prophecy can be the actual conflict

Greek myth gives us the Moirai:
- Clotho;
- Lachesis;
- Atropos.

They are associated with the thread of destiny.

The story uses that cultural image but does NOT require a literal deterministic theology identical to any one ancient source.

Our fiction treats the Loom as:
a divine system through which probable mortal lives become legible to Fate.

The player has no readable thread.

That is the premise.

---

## E. Xenia — hospitality can become world infrastructure

Ancient Greek hospitality carried sacred significance and was associated with Zeus as protector of strangers/guests.

This becomes a beautiful modern-world mechanic.

Certain homes, hotels, cafés, monasteries, hostels and tavernas secretly participate in the **Hearth Road**.

If a traveler is accepted as a guest under divine xenia:
- violence inside the protected threshold becomes extremely dangerous spiritually;
- host must offer basic safety;
- guest must respect the house.

This gives the road-trip story recurring safe spaces without copying a magical camp.

And it creates story when someone violates hospitality.

---

## F. Katabasis — going to the Underworld is a classic heroic act

Greek heroic stories repeatedly use journeys toward or into the realm of the dead.

This world should eventually permit a living player to enter the Underworld for:
- a person;
- knowledge;
- revenge;
- a bargain;
- curiosity;
- death itself.

But it should never be mandatory.

---

## G. Anime progression — earn identity through deeds

Many anime power fantasies work because:
- abilities become personal;
- rivals learn;
- transformations feel earned;
- names/titles acquire weight;
- escalation can become enormous while relationships stay understandable.

THE BLANK PROPHECY uses **Epithets** as that progression surface.

No stat spreadsheet needed.

---

# 3. STORY TITLE

## THE BLANK PROPHECY

Why it works:
- immediately understandable;
- mythic;
- mysterious;
- promises player freedom;
- the “blank” directly supports Plotbreak’s entire product fantasy.

A player sees the title and understands:
**something about me does not fit destiny.**

---

# 4. THE WORLD

Greek mythology is real.

Not exactly as any single surviving text describes it.

That matters because Greek myths themselves contain:
- conflicting genealogies;
- different local traditions;
- contradictory stories;
- gods appearing under different roles/titles.

In this world, those contradictions are not mistakes.

They are a feature.

The gods have accumulated **Names** over thousands of years.

A Name is not merely vocabulary.

It is a way the mortal world understands a god.

When enough belief, story and divine identity gather around a Name, it becomes an **Epithet** with metaphysical weight.

Examples:

**Artemis Agrotera**
the Huntress.

**Athena Polias**
protector of the city.

**Hermes Dolios**
of wiles.

A god can grant a mortal access to one of these Names.

That mortal becomes a **Bearer**.

Most humans never know this world exists.

---

# 5. WHY MYTH IS HIDDEN

There is no giant magical wall erasing every memory.

The hidden world survives because mythic events are:
- rare;
- localized;
- contradictory;
- recorded badly;
- dismissed as stunts, animals, crimes or hallucinations;
- actively cleaned up by divine/mortal institutions when necessary.

Also:

modern phones are not magically useless.

People DO record monsters.

The problem is that:
- a three-second clip proves less than people imagine;
- fake videos are everywhere;
- divine glamour can distort some perception;
- footage gets removed/manipulated by interested parties;
- impossible events are easier to dismiss when viewers did not experience them.

If player deliberately exposes mythology:
the world can become public.

Do not protect secrecy because the premise “requires it.”

---

# 6. THE LOOM OF FATE

Deep beneath Delphi exists a place mythic society calls **the Loom**.

Do not initially explain it as a giant literal textile machine.

At first the player only knows:

Oracles normally perceive a person's life as:
- images;
- symbols;
- threads;
- branching possibilities.

Even powerful gods cannot reliably see every detail.

But there is usually **something**.

The player has nothing.

No image.

No line.

No omen.

No thread.

A prophecy spoken over the player produces blank material.

This is not supposed to happen.

---

# 7. WHAT “BLANK” DOES AND DOES NOT MEAN

Blank does NOT mean:

- player is immortal;
- player cannot suffer consequences;
- player automatically wins;
- player has no past;
- player has no future;
- player is secretly stronger than Zeus.

It means:

**the divine machinery used to read destiny cannot reliably model the player's next choices.**

This has consequences.

## Benefits

- prophecy cannot easily predict player;
- some fate-based curses fail strangely;
- player can sometimes violate “certain” outcomes;
- gods cannot confidently manipulate them using prophecy;
- multiple divine Epithets may coexist in ways usually impossible.

## Costs

- fate-based protections may also fail;
- oracles cannot reassure player;
- certain divine institutions see them as dangerous;
- creatures associated with broken destiny are attracted to them;
- people may project enormous expectations onto them.

The blank is story freedom, not free invincibility.

---

# 8. WHY THE PLAYER IS BLANK

DO NOT fix one answer too early.

The reason should remain **open canon** until the run establishes it.

Possible eventual explanations include:

- born naturally outside the Loom;
- hidden from Fate at birth by a deity;
- descended from a forgotten primordial force;
- came from another world;
- was cut free before memory;
- player’s future self caused the blank;
- a mortal act of impossible choice broke prediction;
- the player authors their own explanation and the world can support it.

The hard canon is ONLY:

**the Loom cannot currently read them.**

This protects Plotbreak freedom.

# 9. THE CENTRAL ANTAGONIST — KYROS ARGYROS

**Age:** 27  
**Role:** former celebrated Bearer / revolutionary / antagonist / possible ally / philosophical rival  
**Public mythic reputation:** “the hero who survived his own prophecy.”

Kyros is the person most interested in the player.

Not because he wants to kill them.

Because they are proof.

---

# 10. KYROS'S HISTORY

At seventeen, Kyros received a prophecy at Delphi.

It said, in plain terms:

> Before his twenty-third year ended, he would kill his younger sister **Mara** with his own hand.

Kyros dedicated six years to preventing it.

He:
- left home;
- refused weapons;
- avoided conflict;
- asked Apollo for guidance;
- studied prophecy;
- isolated himself from Mara.

Every choice moved events toward the same conclusion.

On the final night:
Mara was possessed by a Keres-like spirit of violent death during a mythic outbreak.

She begged Kyros to stop it before it killed others.

Kyros did.

With his own hand.

The prophecy came true.

The divine world treated this as:
tragic inevitability.

Kyros heard:
**you never had a choice.**

That broke him.

---

# 11. KYROS'S BELIEF

Kyros does not think Fate predicts people.

He believes Fate **trains reality toward predictions**.

His argument:

1. An oracle announces an outcome.
2. Gods/mortals act because they know it.
3. Those actions create the outcome.
4. Everyone calls this proof Fate was right.

He wants to destroy the mechanism.

His movement is called:

## THE CUT THREAD

Their belief:

> No child should ever be told the shape of their tragedy before they are old enough to choose a breakfast.

They are not entirely wrong.

---

# 12. KYROS'S PLAN

Kyros is searching for **Atropos's Shears**.

In this story, the Shears are a divine object capable of severing a thread from the Loom.

Historically/mythically, Atropos is associated with the final cutting of life's thread; the story transforms that symbolic role into an actual high-level relic.

Kyros believes that if the Shears are used inside the Loom itself:

**every mortal thread can be cut at once.**

Humanity becomes unreadable.

Free.

The gods lose prophecy.

The Oracles go silent.

---

# 13. THE PROBLEM WITH KYROS'S PLAN

The Loom may do more than predict.

It may stabilize continuity between:
- mortal world;
- Underworld;
- divine promises;
- births;
- deaths;
- oaths.

Nobody fully understands what happens if all threads are severed.

Possible consequences:
- nothing terrible;
- genuine freedom;
- dead souls cannot find their path;
- oaths lose force;
- gods lose domains;
- causality becomes unstable;
- every prophecy dies but so do some protections;
- mythic creatures flood the mortal world.

Kyros knows the risk.

He thinks forced destiny is worse.

The player gets to decide whether he is right.

---

Athens.

The player is at **Monastiraki station** or the surrounding area for whatever reason fits their authored background.

They may be:
- tourist;
- student;
- local;
- worker;

# 15. PROLOGUE

At 11:43 p.m., the final train on Platform 2 arrives six minutes early.

That is the first strange thing.

The second is that nobody else seems bothered by the animal standing on the roof.

It is too large to be a dog.

Too thin to be a lion.

Its front legs bend wrong.

A bronze mask covers its face.

You stare up.

A girl ten meters away sees you staring.

Her expression changes instantly.

“Don’t move.”

Naturally, the creature looks down.

---

# 16. OPENING SCENE — THALIA

The girl is **Thalia Kyrkos**.

Before the player can ask anything:

the creature drops from the train roof.

Its bronze face splits open.

Thalia reaches behind her back and pulls a weapon that absolutely was not there a second ago:

a crescent silver bow with no string.

She looks at the player.

> “You can see it?”
>
> The creature starts running.
>
> “Great. Hate that. Run.”

The player can:
- run;
- fight;
- ask questions;
- protect someone;
- claim they know what the monster is;
- use powers;
- ignore Thalia;
- jump on the train;
- talk to creature;
- do anything.

The story reacts.

---

# 17. THE FIRST MONSTER

The creature is a **Kynokephalic Daemon** inspired by the broad bestiary tradition of Greek composite monsters, but original in design.

Do not pretend it is a famous canonical monster if it is not.

Appearance:
- lion/deer-ish lean body;
- backward-jointed forelimbs;
- bronze funerary mask fused to face;
- black quills along neck;
- eyes visible through mask slits;
- eats strands of fate rather than flesh when possible.

It is attracted to the player because:
there is a hole where their thread should be.

It wants to understand/eat that absence.

This is enough for opening.

Do not explain all of this while it is attacking.

---

# 18. THE HOUSE OF THE STRANGER

If player survives/engages roughly near default route, Thalia brings them to a hidden sanctuary several streets away.

From outside:

a narrow late-night taverna/guesthouse.

Sign:
**TO XENON** / “The Guest.”

Inside:
- mismatched tables;
- old photos;
- travelers;
- kitchen still open;
- people who are clearly not all normal.

This is part of the **Hearth Road**.

---

# 19. XENIA / THE HEARTH ROAD

Across Greece and beyond, certain hosts preserve sacred hospitality.

A person invited across the threshold under **xenia** becomes a guest.

The rules are simple enough to understand:

### Host owes guest
- food/water;
- temporary safety;
- basic truth about immediate danger;
- no betrayal under their own roof.

### Guest owes host
- respect for house;
- no violence against household/other protected guests;
- no theft from host.

Breaking xenia does not automatically summon lightning.

But it creates metaphysical consequences.

Divine promises become easier to enforce around these thresholds.

Some monsters cannot enter.

Others can enter but risk punishment.

The Hearth Road gives travelers safe-ish nodes throughout the story.

Not absolutely safe.

And player can violate the rules.

Then consequences happen.

---

# 20. THE ORACLE TEST

The sanctuary keeper calls **Eleni Vassos**, an elderly modern Oracle.

Eleni does not wear robes.

She arrives annoyed because it is after midnight.

She performs the simplest possible reading over the player using:
- black bowl;
- water;
- burnt laurel;
- a strip of white paper.

For anyone else:
ink-like lines appear.

For the player:

nothing.

Eleni tries again.

Nothing.

The paper burns from the edges inward without producing a word.

Thalia stops joking.

Eleni says:

> “Everyone has something.”
>
> “I don’t?”
>
> “No.”
>
> She looks at you very carefully.
>
> “You have tomorrow. I simply can’t see it.”

Then every flame in the guesthouse goes out.

This is the story's real inciting revelation.

# 21. WHY THE GODS NOTICE

A blank mortal matters because gods rely on prophecy too.

Some react:

## Zeus
Potential threat to cosmic order.

## Athena
Strategic impossibility that must be understood before anyone panics.

## Apollo
Personal/professional humiliation because prophecy is one of his central domains.

## Hermes
Finds it hilarious and potentially useful.

## Artemis
Wants to know why monsters are already hunting player.

## Aphrodite
Sees blank fate as proof that relationships may create outcomes even Fate does not know.

## Hades
Concerned about whether the player’s death will route correctly.

## Hecate
More interested than surprised.

That last reaction is suspicious.

# 22. THALIA KYRKOS

**Age:** 21  
**Role:** first guide / monster hunter / rival-friend / possible romance / independent hero  
**Epithet:** bearer of **Artemis Agrotera — the Huntress**

## Appearance

Striking young Greek woman.

- long dark brown hair usually tied high when fighting;
- olive skin;
- bright grey-green eyes;
- lean athletic build;
- modern black/forest-green streetwear;
- silver crescent earrings;
- practical boots;
- compact backpack.

When invoking Agrotera:
- silver-green markings appear along forearms/eyes;
- crescent bow manifests;
- a short asymmetrical mantle/cloak of spectral forest texture forms behind one shoulder;
- no giant armor suit.

Anime transformation language, grounded clothing.

## Immediate want

Figure out why fate-eating monsters are hunting the player.

## Long-term want

Become good enough that nobody can ever reduce her family to a prophecy again.

## Fear

That she secretly agrees with Kyros.

## Family history

Thalia’s father **Andreas Kyrkos** was a Bearer of Athena.

Years ago an Oracle predicted:
he would betray a sanctuary and cause dozens of deaths.

The Council imprisoned him before he could supposedly do it.

The sanctuary was later attacked anyway.

Andreas died in custody.

No one proved he would have betrayed anybody.

Thalia grew up learning:
prophecy can be used as evidence before a person acts.

This is why Kyros’s ideology bothers her.

## Values
- action over speeches;
- protecting bystanders;
- promises;
- allowing people to prove themselves.

## Boundary
She despises punishing someone for something they have not done yet.

## Secret
Kyros once offered her a place in the Cut Thread.

She considered it for three days.

She has never told her current allies.

## Voice

Fast, dry, contemporary.

She jokes when scared.

She hates mythological exposition.

Example:

> “That was a fate-eater.”
>
> “A what?”
>
> “Big bronze face, tried to eat your destiny. We can workshop the name later.”

## Relationship possibilities

- best friend;
- competitive rival;
- lover;
- fellow traveler;
- ideological enemy;
- person who joins Kyros;
- person player betrays;
- independent hero who leaves;
- dead.

No plot armor.

# 23. THALIA'S EPITHET — ARTEMIS AGROTERA

Historically inspired by Artemis as Huntress.

In this world, Thalia’s bearing manifests as:

### Early capabilities
- spectral bow;
- exceptional tracking;
- can see disturbances left by monsters;
- heightened movement/aim;
- limited ability to sense when prey is lying about direction/escape, NOT mind reading.

### Later growth
The title grows based on deeds.

Possible expressions:
- pack/animal affinity;
- moonlit pursuit;
- long-range impossible shots;
- ability to “mark” a quarry through obstacles;
- protective hunt—finding missing people rather than killing prey.

Do not make a rigid skill tree.

Thalia’s title changes because **her interpretation of “huntress” changes.**

# 24. THE EPITHET SYSTEM

This is the main anime twist.

A god has many Names.

A mortal may carry one.

The mortal becomes a **Bearer**.

The name shapes power.

But the mortal also changes the meaning of the name through how they use it.

---

# 25. HOW A PERSON EARNS AN EPITHET

Not through:
- random loot;
- XP level;
- character creation menu.

A god grants a title because:
- the person did something aligned with that divine aspect;
- the god wants something;
- a ritual occurred;
- an old title lost its previous bearer;
- a deity is desperate.

Some titles are ancient.

Some are new.

This is important:

**The gods can gain new epithets in the modern age.**

If a mortal performs deeds that teach people a new way to understand a god, that meaning can eventually become divine.

This gives Plotbreak absurd freedom.

A player could create:
**Hermes of the Last Train**
as a new modern epithet if their story genuinely earns it.

Do not hand out silly titles constantly.

A new divine Name should feel huge.

---

# 26. THE PLAYER AND EPITHETS

Because the player is blank, normal constraints do not fully apply.

Most Bearers can carry one divine Epithet safely.

The player may eventually:
- carry one;
- reject all;
- exchange one;
- carry multiple;
- combine incompatible divine influences;
- create an entirely new Epithet;
- use self-authored non-divine powers.

This is NOT explained as:
“you have seven ability slots.”

It is story.

# 28. TITLE MANIFESTATION

When invoked, an Epithet creates a temporary **Aspect** around the Bearer.

Avoid copying magical-girl transformation choreography every time.

Visual language:
- markings;
- weapon;
- clothing accents;
- aura motifs;
- symbolic material;
- brief physical transformation.

Examples:

### Hermes Dolios
black-gold winglike light at ankles, coin/road motifs, short cloak, glowing staff/knife depending player.

### Athena Polias
blue-white geometric shield lines resembling city plans, bronze-grey accents, weapon adapted to player.

### Apollo Paian
warm gold-white lines, laurel pattern, healing light through hands/weapon.

### Aphrodite Areia
rose-gold/red warlike halo, elegant armor accents, weapon that reflects player's combat identity rather than “love beam.”

Player style remains central.

---

# 29. SYNCRISIS — COMBINED TITLES

Late-game possibility.

Because player’s fate does not enforce normal identity boundaries, they may combine two Epithets into a temporary **Syncrisis**.

Do not introduce this term early.

Example:
Hermes Dolios + Athena Polias

could become:
a combat style built around citywide misdirection, instant route prediction and impossible pursuit.

Apollo Paian + Aphrodite Areia:
healing through protective battle / turning pain into strength.

This should be generated from actual powers and story.

Not fixed recipes.

---

# 30. GODS ARE CHARACTERS, NOT VENDING MACHINES

A god giving an Epithet does NOT mean:
press button → receive buff.

Gods have:
- wants;
- grudges;
- relationships;
- limits;
- politics.

They can revoke support.

The Epithet itself may remain if the mortal has truly become that meaning.

This creates conflict:
**Does the power belong to the god or to the story people now tell about the Bearer?**

That can become a major late theme.

# 34. ARTEMIS

## Role
Thalia’s patron / wildness / hunt / protector.

She does not “own” Thalia.

Their relationship can be difficult.

## Personality
Direct, unsentimental, protective of those she considers under her responsibility.

She respects player who:
- protects without bragging;
- leaves wild things alone when appropriate;
- pursues relentlessly when needed.
