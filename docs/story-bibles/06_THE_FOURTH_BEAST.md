# THE FOURTH BEAST

**Plotbreak official-world concept**  
**Genre:** creature-bonding adventure / modern fantasy / science-fantasy / mystery / rivalry / action / Paris  
**Core fantasy:** A scientist secretly creates three impossible baby beasts because normal weapons cannot hurt the man terrorizing Paris. You and one rival are the only people he found who can safely bond with them. You choose first.

**One-line hook:** **Three creatures were made to stop the man Paris cannot kill. You get first pick.**

**Secondary reveal hook:** **The professor made three. The killer has a fourth.**

---

# 1. DESIGN NORTH STAR

THE FOURTH BEAST should feel like a premium creature-partner anime unfolding through a real, recognizable Paris.

The player fantasy is:

> **Choose a strange little creature. Bond with it. Watch it grow around the way you treat and fight beside it. Cross Paris chasing a mystery with a rival doing the same thing somewhere else. Get stronger together. Eventually learn that the apparently invincible man you were sent to stop has a magnificent beast of his own.**

This is not primarily a monster-collection spreadsheet.

The first partner matters emotionally.

The player can eventually encounter, rescue, calm, recruit, raise or bond with other creatures if the story goes there, but the starter should remain special because it was the creature that grew up alongside them.

The story needs:
- an immediate starter choice;
- a strong rival;
- cute/beautiful creatures with anime designs;
- visible creature growth;
- Paris as more than a postcard;
- repeated run-ins with the rival without forcing them into the player's party;
- a genuinely frightening main antagonist;
- mystery around why normal weapons fail;
- moments of ordinary city life between fights;
- enough openness that the player can ignore, betray, join or radically transform the intended plot.

**Author the world deeply. Do not author the player's route deeply.**

---

# 2. CREATURE-STORY RESEARCH DNA

This is an original story. Do not copy creature designs, names, capture devices, elemental charts, battle systems, organizations, plot beats or terminology from existing franchises.

The useful high-level lessons are:

## Pokémon — the first choice creates immediate ownership

The first partner choice is powerful because:
- it is simple;
- every option is appealing;
- there is no obviously “wrong” answer;
- the selected creature grows alongside the player;
- the decision gives the player identity immediately.

THE FOURTH BEAST should do this in the first minutes.

The three starters are not a disguised fire/water/grass triangle.

They should offer different personalities and fantasies, not a mathematically obvious counter system.

## Digimon-style partner fiction — evolution is emotional spectacle

Useful principle:
the partner is not merely a collectible unit.

Growth should feel like:
- relationship;
- survival;
- identity;
- dramatic transformation.

A creature's next form can reflect how it has lived with the player rather than being only an XP threshold.

## Monster-rider stories — creatures can change exploration, not just combat

A partner can help the player:
- reach places;
- track people;
- cross environmental obstacles;
- detect things humans cannot;
- move through the city differently.

The creature should feel present outside fights.

## Ni no Kuni / fantastical companion stories — charm matters

A creature world becomes memorable when the companions are:
- expressive;
- funny;
- cute without being useless;
- capable of becoming frighteningly powerful later.

The baby creature should be enjoyable to simply have around.

## Rival-driven anime — the rival should live their own story

The rival does not follow the player as a second party member.

They:
- investigate separately;
- get stronger;
- make mistakes;
- beat the player to clues;
- sometimes need help;
- sometimes save the player;
- can become friend, enemy, lover, collaborator or the person who ultimately solves the case first.

The player should sometimes hear:

> Camille was here three hours ago.

That makes the world feel alive.

# 3. SETTING — PARIS, PRESENT DAY

The story takes place in contemporary Paris.

Do not turn Paris into:
- only Eiffel Tower shots;
- accordion clichés;
- endless luxury boutiques;
- a theme park for tourists.

Use both iconic and lived-in Paris:
- Métro platforms;
- apartment courtyards;
- late-night streets;
- cafés;
- bakeries;
- scooters;
- hospital corridors;
- school/university areas;
- markets;
- bridges;
- rooftops;
- parks;
- staircases;
- service tunnels;
- canal locks;
- crowded public squares;
- ordinary neighborhoods.

Iconic landmarks can become extraordinary because impossible creatures are moving through them.

Paris should feel especially good for this story because it contains:
- dense streets;
- roofs at multiple heights;
- riverbanks;
- underground infrastructure;
- historic stone;
- crowded public spaces;
- old quarries below parts of the city.

A creature chase can go from:
a Métro entrance → narrow street → roof → courtyard → bridge
without feeling like generic fantasy terrain.

# 5. THE WORLD'S CENTRAL MYSTERY

For six months, Paris has experienced a sequence of disappearances and sudden mass-casualty attacks.

The press has given the unknown perpetrator a dozen names.

Police avoid giving him one.

Witness descriptions contradict each other except for several recurring details:

- a man in a pale coat;
- extreme speed;
- red-gold flashes;
- victims thrown or cut with impossible force;
- bullets that appear to miss even at close range;
- security cameras losing several frames at the exact moment he moves.

Some victims disappear completely.

Others are found at scenes that look more like animal attacks than human violence.

No conventional weapon has reliably injured the perpetrator.

Police initially assume:
- armored technology;
- drugs;
- coordinated attackers;
- edited video;
- panic.

Professor **Étienne Morel** does not.

He has seen the biological residue left behind.

It is alive.

---

# 6. PROFESSOR MOREL'S DISCOVERY

Professor Étienne Morel is a synthetic biologist and developmental engineer affiliated with the fictional **Institut Béraud**, a private research institute near the Latin Quarter.

He was brought in quietly after a hospital received tissue recovered from one of the attack scenes.

The material did not match:
- human;
- known animal;
- engineered polymer;
- conventional pathogen.

It behaved like living tissue while repeatedly reorganizing its own structure.

Morel called it **phase tissue** because microscopic portions appeared to occupy slightly different physical states under stress.

He became convinced that the killer's “invulnerability” was not armor.

Something near him was causing impacts to lose clean contact.

Morel tried to reproduce the effect.

He did not build weapons.

He grew animals.

---

# 7. PROJECT CHIMÈRE

Morel combined fragments of phase tissue with synthetic developmental scaffolds and ordinary animal genomic templates.

Most embryos failed.

Three survived.

They are not clones.

They are three completely different living creatures.

Morel refers to them simply as **the Chimère beasts** when speaking scientifically.

In ordinary dialogue:
**beasts**, **partners**, or their names are enough.

Do not make the player memorize a taxonomy.

The three surviving creatures are:

**Aurel**  
**Nox**  
**Marea**

These are Morel's lab names.

The player may rename their chosen beast immediately or later.

If renamed:
the runtime must remember the name permanently.

---

# 8. WHY THE PLAYER AND RIVAL MATTER

The beasts can survive independently.

But their phase tissue becomes unstable under extreme stress unless they form a neurological synchronization with a compatible human.

Morel calls this simply a **bond**.

Do not overexplain it initially.

Morel spent months searching for people whose nervous-system activity matched the pattern.

He found two.

The player.

And **Camille Laurent**.

How the player ended up in Morel's dataset should remain flexible.

Possible explanations:
- volunteered for a harmless research study;
- was treated at a hospital after one of the attacks;
- family connection;
- athletic/neural research;
- self-authored prior connection;
- Morel found them through another route the player establishes.

Camille has a fixed reason:
her older brother **Théo Laurent** disappeared three months ago near one of the attack sites.

Camille agreed immediately.

Paris is starting to get used to memorial candles.

There are some beneath a bridge near the Seine.

More outside a Métro station.

A whole wall of photographs near République.

Nobody agrees on what the man in the security footage is.

A terrorist.

A serial killer.

A military experiment.

A hoax stitched together from unrelated attacks.

Professor Étienne Morel knows one thing the news does not:

**the blood at the crime scenes is not all human.**

Then the story cuts to the player arriving at the Institut Béraud after receiving a message that simply said:

> I believe you can survive something no one else can.

# 10. OPENING SCENE — THE FIRST CHOICE

The player is brought through an ordinary research building and then into a hidden greenhouse-like laboratory beneath it.

Camille Laurent is already there.

She clearly does not know the player.

Three small creatures are waiting in separate open habitats.

No cages.

Morel explains:

> “I made three. I found two people they might accept.”
>
> He looks at the player.
>
> “You arrived first.”
>
> Camille folds her arms.
>
> “Apparently that's how scientific selection works now.”
>
> Morel ignores her.
>
> “Choose.”

The first important player-facing image should show **all three creatures together clearly**.

The image needs to make the choice emotionally difficult.

This is one of the earliest meaningful decisions in the entire story.

# 12. STARTER ONE — AUREL

## Baby design

Aurel looks loosely canine but is not a normal dog.

Key visual identity:
- metallic golden fur;
- enormous fluffy mane around neck/chest;
- silver quills emerging from shoulders and spine;
- teeth completely black, glossy and unnaturally sharp;
- large paws;
- amber eyes;
- expressive ears.

At baby size, Aurel is adorable.

When it bares its teeth, people remember it was created to fight something bullets cannot hurt.

## Personality

Default tendencies:
- social;
- proud;
- physically affectionate when bonded;
- protective;
- jealous of attention;
- loves running;
- dislikes being confined.

These are tendencies, not absolute commands.

The player's treatment can change its behavior.

## Core abilities

Early:
- extraordinary acceleration;
- scent/tracking beyond ordinary animals;
- silver quills harden under threat;
- bite can physically contact phase-shifted targets;
- instinctive protection of bonded human.

Later possibilities:
- quills can flare outward into defensive armor;
- spikes can detach/project under stress;
- mane stores and releases impact/heat;
- extreme pursuit speed;
- protective phase barrier.

## Growth philosophy

Aurel's mature form should reflect whether its bond developed mainly through:
- protection;
- pursuit;
- aggression;
- companionship;
- disciplined combat;
- some strange route the player creates.

### Guardian-leaning later form
Broader shoulders, enormous luminous gold mane, silver quills form layered shield-like crown around upper body, barrier/impact-redirection abilities.

### Hunter-leaning later form
Leaner, longer-legged, mane swept backward, black teeth more pronounced, silver quills narrower and projectile-like, terrifying acceleration.

Do NOT force only these two branches.

They are authored seeds.

The AI may create a coherent new form from the player's actual history.

# 13. STARTER TWO — NOX

## Baby design

Nox is a squat black salamander-like creature.

Key visual identity:
- charcoal-black skin/scales;
- glowing red circular markings along back, sides and tail;
- fierce orange-red eyes;
- broad head;
- small sharp teeth;
- long expressive tail;
- very low center of gravity.

Nox looks permanently annoyed.

It is the smallest of the three at story start.

## Personality

Default tendencies:
- suspicious;
- fiercely loyal once trust is earned;
- hates being picked up without permission;
- curious about heat, machinery and food;
- stares at strangers rather than hiding;
- more independent than Aurel.

## Core abilities

Early:
- climbs walls/ceilings;
- regenerates minor wounds unusually quickly;
- red markings absorb heat and kinetic energy;
- can discharge stored energy in short bursts;
- can bite phase targets.

Later:
- thermal shock;
- explosive movement;
- extreme regeneration;
- smoke/heat distortion;
- temporary camouflage against sensors;
- larger armored body.

### Furnace-leaning later form
Thicker black plated body, red nodes connect into luminous channels, powerful stored-energy releases, high durability.

### Shade-leaning later form
Longer and sleeker, almost dragonlike-salamander silhouette but still clearly amphibian, dimmer nodes, wall/ceiling speed, heat-haze concealment, ambush focus.

Again:
forms should adapt to the actual story rather than operate as a rigid videogame tree.

# 14. STARTER THREE — MAREA

## Baby design

Marea is the strangest of the three.

It resembles a tiny terrestrial orca built to live on land.

Key identity:
- coral-pink / rose-red upper body;
- cream markings around face, underside and eyes;
- compact four-legged body;
- small dorsal fin;
- powerful rounded tail;
- glossy black eyes;
- broad expressive mouth;
- extremely smooth body surface.

Its legs should feel evolutionarily coherent with the orca-like body, not like a whale pasted onto dog legs.

Anime creature design, not realism.

## Personality

Default tendencies:
- playful;
- extremely curious;
- steals objects;
- loves physical games;
- bonds quickly;
- hates prolonged stillness;
- surprisingly sensitive to human emotion.

## Core abilities

Early:
- echolocation through walls;
- detects movement and hidden spaces;
- powerful short-range pressure pulse;
- incredible tackling force;
- can move unusually smoothly over pavement and walls.

Later:
Marea begins interacting with solid surfaces as though they have current.

It can partially **phase-swim** through stone, concrete and other dense material.

This should initially be limited and visually spectacular rather than a universal teleport button.

### Breaker-leaning later form
Heavier, muscular terrestrial orca, larger dorsal crest, pressure-wave charge attacks, ground-splitting impact, powerful sonar.

### Current-leaning later form
Sleeker body, longer fin structures, can briefly “swim” through walls/ground, emerge from surfaces, map underground spaces through echolocation.

This makes Marea uniquely valuable in Paris's tunnels.

This is NOT a stat card.

Do not show:

Attack 8
Defense 6
Speed 7

Instead, the three ready-to-play responses should communicate personality/fantasy.

### Choose Aurel

### Choose Nox
> I ignore the fact that the salamander looks like it wants to bite me and hold out my hand anyway. “You and me are going to get along terribly. I can already tell.”

### Choose Marea
> I stare at the pink land-orca for a long second. “I have absolutely no idea what you're supposed to be.” I kneel beside it. “So obviously I'm picking you.”

- “I choose none of them.”

The story adapts.

# 17. THE RIVAL CHOOSES SECOND

Camille chooses one of the remaining beasts after the player.

Default pairing for replayable contrast:

- Player chooses Aurel → Camille chooses Nox.
- Player chooses Nox → Camille chooses Marea.
- Player chooses Marea → Camille chooses Aurel.

The remaining third beast stays with Morel initially.

This is not because of a hidden rock-paper-scissors counter system.

It simply gives different runs different rival imagery and tactics.

If the player does something that makes this sequence nonsensical, adapt.

Examples:
- player convinces Camille to choose first;
- player tries to claim all three;
- one beast rejects somebody;
- player refuses any bond.

Do not defend the script.

## Immediate want

Find her missing brother Théo.

## Long-term want

Be the person who actually stops the abductions rather than waiting for institutions that failed her family.

## Fear

## Values

- loyalty;
- competence;
- keeping promises;

## Boundary

## Secret

She does not trust the professor completely.

## Voice

Contemporary.

Direct.

Quick sarcasm.

Not excessive French clichés.

She rarely makes speeches.

## Rival behavior

- sometimes gets injured;

## Relationship possibilities

- intense competitive rivalry;
- sibling-like friendship;
- mutual respect;
- romance;

# 20. PROFESSOR ÉTIENNE MOREL

**Age:** 47.

**Role:** scientist / creator of three beasts / source of knowledge / morally gray mentor.

**Appearance:** lean French man, prematurely silver hair, tired blue-grey eyes, dark sweater under lab coat, rolled sleeves, glasses used only for close work, looks more like someone who has slept in his lab for six months than a cartoon professor.

## Immediate want

Prove the beasts can stop the killer.

## Long-term want

Understand what the phase tissue actually is before governments turn it into weapons.

## Fear

That he created three intelligent lives solely because he needed weapons.

## Values
- curiosity;
- life;
- evidence;
- personal responsibility.

## Major flaw

He rationalizes secrecy.

He repeatedly tells himself:

> I'll tell them when I understand more.

This creates legitimate mistrust.

## Secret

Morel did not create the phase tissue.

He recovered it from a crime scene.

And one of the earliest samples contained a complete hair follicle from a large white feline.

He has suspected for months that the killer may have an animal like the three beasts.

He does NOT know exactly what it is.

He did not tell player or Camille because he feared they would refuse.

## Voice

Clear, tired, literal.

He explains difficult science in normal language.

Bad:
> “The paracausal bioresonant membrane exists within an adjacent ontological frequency.”

Good:
> “Bullets aren't bouncing off him. For a fraction of a second, they aren't touching him at all.”

Only later:
> “I call that phase displacement.”

Concrete before abstract.

# 23. THE ANTAGONIST — LUCIEN MAROT

**Age:** 34.

**Public identity:** biomedical entrepreneur and philanthropist associated with a private regenerative-medicine foundation.

**True role:** the man behind the abductions and attacks.

**Appearance:** extremely composed, elegant, black hair brushed back, dark eyes, usually pale grey/cream coat, physically unremarkable until he moves.

Do not make him look obviously evil in public.

## Personality

Lucien is frightening because he is:
- patient;
- curious;
- convinced;
- capable of enormous violence without emotional frenzy.

He is not constantly screaming.

He can have normal conversations.

He genuinely likes animals more than most people.

## Immediate want

Locate humans capable of stable phase bonds.

## Long-term want

Open a permanent biological passage between Earth and the ecosystem the phase tissue comes from.

## Belief

Human civilization has built a world that excludes every living thing it cannot domesticate.

He sees the other ecology as:
- evolution;
- liberation;
- a second biosphere;
- something humanity has no right to keep out.

His conclusion is monstrous:

> Paris should be forced to adapt.

## Why abduct people?

Exposure to phase tissue produces rare neurological changes in some humans.

Lucien abducts people exposed during prior incidents and tests whether they can sustain a bond.

Most cannot.

Some die during experimentation.

Others he kills once they become liabilities.

The mass attacks serve multiple purposes:
- field-test Alba;
- create new exposed survivors;
- overwhelm investigation;
- force the city into fear;
- collect biological data.

He is responsible.

Do not soften this into “misunderstood good guy.”

The player may still join him.

## Personal connection to Morel

Lucien funded an early stage of Morel's regenerative research through intermediaries.

Morel did not knowingly work on the abductions.

Lucien has known about Project Chimère longer than Morel realizes.

# 24. THE FOURTH BEAST — ALBA

Later reveal.

Lucien has a beast.

Unlike Aurel, Nox and Marea:

**Morel did not create Alba.**

His three beasts were reverse-engineered from biological traces originating from Alba.

Alba is the reason the synthetic beasts can hurt Lucien.

## Appearance

A breathtaking white cheetah-like creature.

Not a realistic cheetah.

Anime/mythic design.

Key identity:
- pristine luminous white coat;
- extremely sleek long-limbed feline body;
- subtle red-gold gradients around shoulders, tail tip and eyes;
- faint luminous markings like sunrise through marble;
- razor-black claws with red-gold edge;
- black teeth with faint gold root glow;
- long elegant tail;
- eyes red-gold;
- unnaturally quiet movement.

It should look:
**beautiful enough that seeing it for the first time briefly interrupts the fear.**

## Size

At rest:
roughly large-cheetah / small-big-cat scale.

Under combat phase:
its proportions can stretch subtly, making it larger and more mythic.

Do not turn it into a giant lion.

## Personality

Alba is not Lucien's robot.

It:
- chooses;
- observes;
- dislikes unnecessary noise;
- hunts with terrifying patience;
- can disobey Lucien;
- appears genuinely attached to him.

This matters later.

## Abilities

- extreme acceleration;
- “skip-step” movement where cameras lose frames;
- phase sheath that prevents ordinary impacts from contacting Lucien cleanly;
- claws that cut phase-active tissue;
- can disappear from ordinary perception briefly while moving;
- tracks bonded beasts across surprising distances.

Alba is NOT literally teleporting at first.

It is moving through a neighboring physical state for fractions of a second.

## Critical reveal image

The player should eventually see Lucien standing in a quiet Paris location with Alba beside him.

The image must feel like:
**oh fuck, he has one too.**

# 26. WHAT REGULAR WEAPONS ACTUALLY DO

This must be understandable.

Lucien is not Superman.

Alba produces a thin phase field around him under threat.

A bullet reaches the field.

For a fraction of a second, the bullet and Lucien do not occupy compatible physical states.

The bullet continues past or deforms unpredictably.

To witnesses:
he appears bulletproof.

The Chimère beasts contain copied phase tissue.

They can physically touch Alba and Lucien through that field.

This is why Morel created living creatures rather than another gun.

Later:
the player may develop technology or powers that also interact with the phase state.

Do not hard-lock the entire plot to beasts being the only solution forever.

# 27. THE OTHER ECOLOGY

This is where the isekai flavor becomes larger.

Alba is not a normal Earth organism.

Its biology originated in an adjacent ecological space Morel eventually calls **the Elsewhere** in plain conversation.

Do not introduce the term in turn one.

The deeper truth:

beneath parts of Paris, natural geological structures allow extremely brief overlap with another biosphere.

The environment on the other side is not “fantasy Paris.”

It is a vast wild ecology occupying analogous physical space.

Where Paris has:
- limestone;
- tunnels;
- river;
- hills;

the Elsewhere may contain:
- luminous mineral forests;
- enormous roots;
- bioluminescent rivers;
- predatory plains;
- impossible animal life.

The Catacombs/quarries are where the boundary is thinnest.

Lucien found Alba there years ago.

Whether Alba crossed willingly, was stranded, or was born during an overlap can remain discoverable.

## Important

Do not force the player into the Elsewhere.

It is available as:
- mystery;
- endgame region;
- exploration route;
- source of new beasts.

A player can stay entirely in Paris and still have a complete story.

After player chooses, Morel does not inject a chip or hand them a capture device.

He opens the habitat.

The creature decides whether to approach.

The bond begins through direct interaction.

Possible:
- Aurel sniffs player's hand then climbs directly onto them.
- Nox bites lightly, then refuses to let go of sleeve.
- Marea circles player, bumps them hard enough to nearly knock them over.

The player's response matters.

Morel:

> “Don't perform for it.”
>
> “What?”
>
> “It isn't choosing your résumé.”

This keeps the moment about personality.

# 71. THE RIVAL DOES NOT EXIST TO LOSE

Camille can beat the player.

She can:
- win a duel;
- solve clue first;
- save victim first;
- evolve first;
- expose Lucien first;
- even defeat Lucien if the player abandons the central conflict long enough and her route plausibly succeeds.

If Camille “wins the story,” the player can continue their own life afterward.

Do not rubber-band her below the protagonist.

# 72. USER CAN RUIN THE PREMISE

Examples:

## “I kill Morel immediately.”
He can die.
The beasts still exist.
Camille reacts.
The player may have to figure everything out without him.

## “I set all three beasts free.”
They scatter or stay based on behavior.
Story becomes about what follows.

## “I call the police.”
Inès becomes important much earlier.

## “I go public immediately.”
The secret collapses.

## “I join Lucien.”
Then the story becomes villain-side creature fantasy.

## “I leave Paris.”
The story can travel.
Camille and Lucien continue acting in Paris unless circumstances change.

## “I am already stronger than Lucien.”
Accept if compatible.
The story's challenge may shift toward:
finding victims, understanding Elsewhere, rival conflict, political consequences.

Do not secretly weaken the player to preserve boss difficulty.

**CURRENT OBJECTIVE: GO TO CANAL SAINT-MARTIN**

# 84. ENDINGS — ELIGIBILITY, NOT ROUTES

## PARIS, STILL OURS — Standard
**Eligibility:** Lucien's immediate killing operation is stopped and Paris remains mostly ordinary, regardless of whether every mystery is solved.

**Epilogue direction:** everyday city resumes around whatever life player built with their beast.

---

## THE FOURTH BEAST — Rare
**Eligibility:** Alba survives, Lucien's relationship to it ends, and player/Camille/another meaningful character forms a new legitimate relationship with Alba.

**Epilogue:** the “fourth” creature is no longer a villain's weapon.

---

## TWO HANDLERS — Rare
**Eligibility:** player and Camille both survive, complete major arcs, and end with deep mutual respect regardless of romance.

**Epilogue:** they continue separately but remain each other's benchmark.

---

## MORE THAN RIVALS — Rare
**Eligibility:** player/Camille relationship becomes genuinely romantic and survives the main crisis.

**Epilogue:** should reflect their actual dynamic, not generic marriage.

---

## THE PROFESSOR'S MENAGERIE — Rare
**Eligibility:** Morel survives and the creatures become part of an ethical protected research/partnership program with player support.

**Epilogue:** new compatible people meet creatures under radically different rules.

---

## NO MASTERS — Rare
**Eligibility:** player rejects human ownership/control framing and frees or protects phase creatures as independent beings.

**Epilogue:** beasts become a political/legal/ecological question rather than weapons.

---

## WILD PARIS — Hidden
**Eligibility:** Elsewhere permanently overlaps with Paris in a stable enough form that the city survives but transforms.

**Epilogue:** vines/mineral forests/creatures coexist with Métro, rooftops and ordinary life.

---

## ACROSS THE VEIL — Hidden
**Eligibility:** player willingly leaves Earth/Paris for Elsewhere.

**Epilogue:** chosen beast returns to its biological origin with the player.

---

## THE NEW PREDATOR — Dark
**Eligibility:** player joins/takes over Lucien's operation or becomes the dominant phase-bonded threat in Paris.

**Epilogue:** authorities learn to fear a different name.

---

## CAMILLE WINS — Standard/Rare
**Eligibility:** player abandons or fails central conflict while Camille plausibly succeeds in stopping Lucien.

**Epilogue:** the world did not wait for the protagonist.

Player's own life remains reflected.

---

## THÉO COMES HOME — Rare
**Eligibility:** Théo survives and is genuinely recovered/reintegrated.

**Epilogue:** Camille's story gets emotional closure without requiring player romance.

---

## EMPTY LAB — Dark
**Eligibility:** Morel and the original three beasts are lost/destroyed/dispersed, and player leaves the phase conflict unresolved.

**Epilogue:** one final unexplained creature sighting in Paris.

---

## LUCIEN'S PARIS — Dark
**Eligibility:** Lucien successfully opens the boundary and retains control/influence over the aftermath.

**Epilogue:** Paris adapts under his ideology.

Not necessarily game over.

---

## JUST US — Standard
**Eligibility:** player walks away from conspiracy and builds another life with their chosen beast.

**Epilogue:** perhaps a quieter city, perhaps not; the beast remains their companion.

A legitimate ending.

# 101. STORY QUALITY BAR

After 30 minutes, a player should remember:

- which beast they chose;
- what that beast is like;
- Camille;
- the fact that someone in Paris cannot be killed by normal weapons;
- at least one specific Paris location;
- at least one moment where their beast behaved like a character;
- the feeling that their beast is going to become something much more powerful.

After longer play, they should remember the first time they saw Alba.

That reveal must land.

# 102. FINAL CREATIVE BAR

Different players should be able to have radically different THE FOURTH BEAST stories.

### Player 1
Chooses Aurel, becomes protective hero, falls for Camille, stops Lucien and keeps Elsewhere secret.

### Player 2
Chooses Nox, hates Camille, joins Lucien, turns Paris into a hybrid ecosystem.

### Player 3
Chooses Marea, ignores Lucien for twenty turns, becomes famous online for riding a pink land-orca through Paris, then gets dragged back into the crisis when their friend disappears.

### Player 4
Refuses all three, works with Inès as investigator, eventually bonds with a wild creature.

### Player 5
Claims they are from Elsewhere, and the entire scientific understanding of the story changes around them.

### Player 6
Kills Lucien early, then spends the rest of the story dealing with Alba, the Fold and a collapsing boundary.

### Player 7
Lets Camille solve the case while they build a quiet life with their creature.

All seven should still feel unmistakably like **THE FOURTH BEAST**.

Because the identity comes from:
- Paris;
- Morel's three creations;
- Camille's parallel journey;
- Alba;
- the phase mystery;
- the bond between human and beast.

Not from forcing the player down one route.
