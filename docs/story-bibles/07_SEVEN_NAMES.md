# SEVEN NAMES

**Plotbreak official-world concept**  
**Genre:** Belle Époque revenge / gentleman-thief adventure / conspiracy thriller / romance / duels / social infiltration  
**Setting:** France, 1909 — primarily Paris, with Marseille, Lyon and the Côte d’Azur available as the story expands  
**Core fantasy:** You were condemned for the assassination of a beloved minister. The night before you are meant to die in a fortress prison, a hole opens in your cell wall. A dying prisoner gives you an escape route, the key to a hidden fortune, and seven names tied to the night your life was destroyed.

**One-line hook:** **They stole your name. A dying prisoner gives you seven.**

**Longer player-facing hook:**  
You were convicted of murdering a minister in front of half of Paris. Tomorrow morning, you die for it. Tonight, the old prisoner in the next cell breaks through your wall and gives you seven names. Banker. General. Doctor. Newspaper owner. Police chief. Industrialist. Politician. He says every one of them touched the night you were condemned.

Escape. Clear your name. Destroy theirs. Steal everything. Forgive them. Join them.

France is about to learn you survived.

---

# 1. WHY THIS STORY EXISTS

SEVEN NAMES is designed specifically from patterns that have repeatedly worked in French popular storytelling across very different eras.

It should not copy any one work.

Instead, it combines several durable fantasies that French audiences have historically embraced and that remain commercially visible now:

- **revenge and reinvention**;
- **the gentleman thief / charming outlaw**;
- **swashbuckling friendship, duels and political intrigue**;
- **large conspiracies hidden beneath respectable institutions**;
- **adventure with humor and personality rather than relentless grimness**;
- **secret identities and double lives**;
- **a crew of strong recurring characters**;
- **modern manga-style emotional momentum and progression**;
- **a player who can become increasingly legendary without filling out a stat spreadsheet**.

This is not “a French literature adaptation.”

It is an original playable-anime world built from story instincts that have proven unusually durable in France.

# 2. RESEARCH FOUNDATION — WHAT FRANCE KEEPS RESPONDING TO

This section exists so the eventual implementation understands WHY the concept is built this way.

Do not reproduce copyrighted plots, characters, designs or proprietary terminology from contemporary works.

## A. Alexandre Dumas — revenge, identity, friendship, velocity

Two especially durable French adventure fantasies matter here:

### The Count of Monte Cristo
Useful high-level ingredients:
- wrongful imprisonment or catastrophic betrayal;
- escape;
- reinvention;
- money/access changing the protagonist’s reach;
- revenge against a network rather than one villain;
- discovering that vengeance becomes morally complicated once enemies become human beings;
- multiple identities;
- long-term consequences.

The modern French appetite for this material is clearly not merely academic or nostalgic. The 2024 French film adaptation became one of the country’s major theatrical successes.

### The Three Musketeers
Useful ingredients:
- fast-moving adventure;
- friendship/crew chemistry;
- swordplay;
- political conspiracy;
- seductive dangerous social worlds;
- characters with dramatically different personalities working together;
- a protagonist who can enter a larger story without understanding all of it first.

The recent French two-film adaptation also drew a major audience.

### Plotbreak lesson
Revenge gives the player motivation.

A crew gives them people to care about.

Neither should dictate the route.

---

## B. Arsène Lupin — being clever can be the power fantasy

French popular fiction has an unusually strong native gentleman-thief tradition.

Useful ingredients:
- disguises;
- audacity;
- elegance;
- social engineering;
- puzzles;
- theft as performance;
- outsmarting authority;
- becoming famous under an alias;
- humor and panache rather than purely grim crime;
- the same protagonist functioning as criminal, investigator and adventurer.

### Plotbreak lesson

The player should be able to win a “boss encounter” by:

- stealing a letter;
- replacing a painting;
- seducing the wrong person;
- forging an invitation;
- walking into a gala under a fake identity;
- publicly humiliating an enemy;
- convincing the police they are somewhere else;

rather than only fighting.

**Intelligence and nerve should feel as powerful as a sword.**

---

## C. Astérix — personality, companionship, humor, travel

Astérix remains commercially enormous.

The relevant lesson is not “make Gauls.”

It is:
- adventure is more memorable when characters are funny;
- close companions with contrasting personalities create replayable chemistry;
- travel works when each place has a social identity;
- serious danger can coexist with playful energy;
- audiences like heroes who make powerful institutions look ridiculous.

### Plotbreak lesson

SEVEN NAMES should not become 40 turns of rain, betrayal and tortured monologues.

A disastrous disguise, terrible hotel room, champagne theft, arrogant aristocrat, annoyed horse, failed seduction attempt or friend making fun of the player can be as important to attachment as a conspiracy reveal.

---

## D. Blake & Mortimer / Franco-Belgian adventure tradition — mysteries with scale

The continued strength of classic adventure/mystery BD in France shows the durability of:
- investigations;
- elaborate schemes;
- hidden technology;
- historical secrets;
- respectable institutions concealing danger;
- travel;
- “what the hell is really going on?” momentum.

### Plotbreak lesson

Each of the seven names should open a human mystery.

Do not make them seven videogame bosses.

---

## E. One Piece — freedom, crew, reputation

One Piece continues to place multiple volumes among France’s best-selling manga.

Useful high-level ingredients:
- large adventure space;
- found family;
- memorable rivals;
- personal dreams;
- escalating reputation;
- the world reacts to the protagonist becoming important;
- locations have distinct identities;
- the protagonist can create history rather than merely uncover it.

### Plotbreak lesson

The player’s **legend** can be the progression system.

At first:
nobody believes the condemned prisoner survived.

Later:
people whisper an alias.

Eventually:
newspapers print it.

Enemies change plans because of it.

That feels like progression without:
**REPUTATION +5**

---

## F. Spy x Family — secret identities plus warm interpersonal stakes

Spy x Family’s continued French sales support another useful principle:

A conspiracy story can remain emotionally light and character-driven.

Secret identities are especially fun when:
- people care about each other outside the mission;
- lies create comedy as well as danger;
- domestic/social scenes give espionage meaning.

### Plotbreak lesson

The player’s false identity should create:
- friendships;
- romance;
- awkwardness;
- obligations;
- people they do not want to deceive anymore.

---

## G. French-made manga such as Radiant and Instinct — manga grammar can feel French

France does not only import manga.

Successful French creators have shown that manga pacing and visual language can blend naturally with:
- European settings;
- European history/folklore;
- contemporary French sensibility;
- social themes.

Instinct’s breakout performance is especially useful evidence that a young French audience will embrace an original French-authored shōnen/thriller if the hook and emotional engine work.

### Plotbreak lesson

Do not “translate Japanese anime into France.”

Create an anime-like story whose cultural bones already make sense in France.

---

## H. Solo Leveling / progression fantasy — visible personal ascent

Solo Leveling’s large French audience reinforces the appeal of:
- starting in a compromised position;
- rapid personal ascent;
- increasingly large consequences;
- becoming someone the world can no longer ignore.

### Plotbreak lesson

SEVEN NAMES needs progression.

But progression is:
- identities;
- influence;
- allies;
- skills;
- wealth;
- access;
- reputation;
- secrets;
- leverage.

Not necessarily levels.

The year is 1909.

Use a lightly heightened but recognizable Belle Époque France.

The world contains:
- electric lighting in wealthy/public spaces;
- telephones;
- telegraph;
- newspapers with enormous cultural influence;
- early automobiles alongside horses;
- trains linking major cities;
- photography;
- grand hotels;
- theaters;
- cafés;
- cabarets;
- department stores;
- aristocratic salons;
- industrial wealth;
- political scandal;
- striking economic inequality.

Do NOT turn this into generic steampunk.

No mandatory:
- goggles;
- brass robots;
- airships everywhere;
- Victorian-English aesthetic.

This is France.

Technology should feel like the beginning of modernity, not retro-futurist cosplay.

# 6. OPENING HARD FACTS

At story start, only a small set of facts must be fixed:

1. The player has been convicted of assassinating **Minister Jules Valère**.
2. Valère was killed at a masked charity gala in Paris eleven months earlier.
3. Public evidence against the player appeared overwhelming.
4. The player has spent most of those eleven months in **Fort Serein**, an island prison off the Mediterranean coast.
5. The player is scheduled to die the next morning.
6. The old prisoner in the neighboring cell has been tunneling for years.
7. He possesses information connecting seven powerful people to the Valère affair.

### IMPORTANT

Do NOT hard-canon:

**THE PLAYER IS INNOCENT.**

The player can say:
- “I was framed.”
- “I killed him.”
- “I helped.”
- “I don’t remember.”
- “The wrong person died.”
- something stranger.

The conspiracy truth should adapt around that without breaking the fixed facts that:
- Valère is dead;
- the player was convicted;
- seven people were involved in the surrounding conspiracy.

This gives significantly more freedom than a standard revenge novel.

# 7. PROLOGUE

Tomorrow morning, France expects you to die.

Eleven months ago, Minister Jules Valère collapsed beneath the glass ceiling of the Hôtel Beaumont with a knife in his chest and your name on every front page by sunrise.

Witnesses placed you beside him.

A revolver registered to you was found in the service corridor.

Letters in your handwriting described the assassination weeks before it happened.

The prosecution called the case airtight.

By winter, people stopped arguing about whether you did it.

They started arguing about whether the execution should be public.

Fort Serein sits alone in black Mediterranean water.

Your cell has one bed.

One narrow window.

And, tonight, a hole opening in the wall.

# 8. OPENING SCENE

Stone dust spills across the floor at 01:17.

A brick drops inward.

Then another.

A thin old man crawls through the wall from the neighboring cell, bleeding through his shirt and trying not to laugh.

This is **Marcel Bellac**, Prisoner 47.

You have heard him coughing through the wall for months.

You have never seen his face.

He puts three things on your floor:

- an iron key;
- a folded railway map;
- a piece of paper containing seven names.

Then he says:

> “If you want to spend your last six hours proving you’re innocent, be my guest.”
>
> He looks toward the door.
>
> “If you want to survive them, we should leave.”

The player can:
- escape;
- refuse;
- demand answers;
- attack Marcel;
- admit guilt;
- ask about one of the names;
- call the guard;
- claim they have their own escape plan;
- use a self-authored ability;
- do anything else.

# 9. MARCEL BELLAC

**Age:** 63  
**Role:** old prisoner / former forger / conspiracy witness / possible mentor if he survives.

## Who he was

Marcel Bellac was once one of France’s finest document forgers.

Not because he imitated handwriting.

Because he understood institutions.

He could manufacture:
- bank histories;
- family records;
- military orders;
- property transfers;
- correspondence;
- entire identities.

For nearly twenty years, Marcel worked for powerful people who needed inconvenient facts to become convenient ones.

He helped maintain something called **the Registry**.

Then he tried to leave.

Fort Serein received him seven years ago.

Official charge:
treason.

## Immediate want
Escape before his untreated internal injury kills him.

## Long-term want
Make sure the Registry cannot continue existing unchanged.

## Fear
That the player will become exactly like the people on the list once given enough money and power.

## Secret
Marcel personally forged two pieces of evidence used against the player.

He did not know at the time who would be condemned.

He recognized the case only after the player arrived at Fort Serein.

This is why he finally accelerated the escape tunnel.

## Voice

Dry.

Uns sentimental.

Enjoys making dramatic ideas sound practical.

Example:

> “Revenge is expensive.”
>
> “How expensive?”
>
> “Depends how tasteful you are.”

## Survival

Do NOT force Marcel to die in the opening.

He is injured.

Depending on player:
- he escapes with them;
- dies during escape;
- stays behind;
- is betrayed;
- receives medical treatment;
- becomes long-term adviser;
- is handed to authorities.

His death is not required for drama.

---

# 10. THE SEVEN NAMES

The paper contains seven names.

Marcel tells the player:

> “Do not make the beginner’s mistake.”
>
> “What mistake?”
>
> “Assuming a name on a list means the person deserves to die.”

Each person touched the Valère conspiracy differently.

They are not seven equal villains.

They are seven doors.

# 11. NAME ONE — HENRI VARENNE

**Age:** 55  
**Public role:** financier; chairman of Banque Varenne; railway investor.  
**Connection:** financed the private intelligence network surrounding the Registry.

## Public reputation
Elegant, philanthropic, serious.

He funds:
- hospitals;
- museums;
- scholarships;
- infrastructure.

## Truth
Henri believed the Registry could prevent political chaos by ensuring powerful people always had something to lose.

He did NOT initially approve Valère’s assassination.

Once the murder happened, he helped finance the cover-up because exposure would implicate his family and bank.

## Personal complication
Henri is the father of **Céleste Varenne**.

## Possible player relationship
- enemy;
- reluctant source;
- patron;
- blackmail target;
- surrogate father figure;
- murder victim;
- business partner.

Henri should occasionally make disturbingly good arguments for stability.

---

# 12. NAME TWO — SOLÈNE ARTOIS

**Age:** 44  
**Role:** owner of newspaper empire **Le Matin Français** and several regional papers.

## Connection
She turned the player from suspect into national monster before trial.

## Philosophy
People imagine newspapers report public opinion.

Solène believes newspapers manufacture the conditions under which public opinion becomes possible.

## Secret
She possesses an unpublished photograph from the gala showing someone entering the service corridor after the player supposedly left.

She never printed it.

## Why?
Because the Registry held evidence that could destroy her younger sister’s family.

## Character possibility
Solène can be:
- cruel adversary;
- valuable ally;
- person who publicly clears player;
- person player replaces as media power.

---

# 13. NAME THREE — PREFECT ARMAND DURET

**Age:** 52  
**Role:** senior police authority in Paris.

## Connection
Oversaw the investigation.

## Truth
Duret knowingly allowed planted evidence into the case.

## Motivation
He believed Valère’s proposed reforms would expose dozens of undercover operations and destroy public trust in the police.

He saw sacrificing one defendant as protecting an institution.

## Personality
Blunt.

Institutionalist.

Not charming.

## Pressure
If he learns player survived, he becomes one of the most immediately dangerous names.

---

# 14. NAME FOUR — DR. ANAÏS BELLIER

**Age:** 39  
**Role:** physician / forensic specialist.

## Connection
Signed the official forensic chronology placing Valère’s death inside the narrow window that made the prosecution’s timeline work.

## Truth
Her report was false.

## Why?
Her son had been kidnapped for forty-eight hours.

He was returned unharmed after she signed.

Anaïs has lived with the guilt since.

## Important
She is the strongest proof that:

**the list is not a kill list.**

If the player approaches her violently, they may never learn what happened.

If approached differently, she can become:
- witness;
- medical ally;
- friend;
- enemy terrified for her family.

---

# 15. NAME FIVE — GENERAL BASTIEN LORCQ

**Age:** 58  
**Role:** military intelligence figure.

## Connection
Provided the false chain of evidence showing the player had access to restricted weapons.

## Belief
France is entering an era where private money, newspapers and radicals can destabilize governments faster than armies can respond.

He believes secret coercive networks are ugly but necessary.

## Secret
He kept a second archive because he never trusted the other six.

His archive can potentially destroy everyone.

---

# 16. NAME SIX — LUCIEN MAUREL

**Age:** 47  
**Role:** industrialist; shipping, steel, weapons manufacturing.

## Connection
Provided the logistics used by Valère’s real killers.

## Personality
Charming in the way someone becomes charming after twenty years of getting whatever they want.

## Truth
Unlike several others:

Lucien knew an assassination was planned.

He helped willingly.

## Reason
Valère intended to cancel a defense contract that would have ruined part of Maurel’s empire and exposed bribery.

Lucien is one of the clearest legitimate revenge targets.

Still:
the player can ally with him.

# 17. NAME SEVEN — ÉTIENNE VEYRAC

**Age:** 41  
**Public role:** former chief of staff to Jules Valère; now rising reform politician.

This name should surprise anyone familiar with the public case.

Veyrac was Valère’s closest political ally.

He gave the emotional eulogy.

He testified against the player reluctantly and became nationally admired for “putting truth above friendship.”

## Authored truth

Veyrac orchestrated the assassination.

Not because he hated Valère.

Because Valère intended to expose the Registry publicly.

Veyrac believed publication would:
- destroy courts;
- collapse banks;
- ruin innocent families;
- reveal military operations;
- create political chaos.

Veyrac decided Valère had become more dangerous than the corruption he wanted to expose.

Then he realized the crisis could also create his own political ascent.

That second decision is what makes him the central antagonist rather than a tragic pragmatist.

## Important

Veyrac should not sit in a castle waiting to be the final boss.

He:
- gives speeches;
- builds coalitions;
- reacts to rumors;
- investigates player;
- attempts negotiation;
- can offer amnesty;
- can become ally under bizarre routes;
- can be killed early;
- can lose office;
- can win national power.

If killed early:
the Registry and the other six still exist.

The story continues.

# 18. THE REGISTRY

The Registry is the central conspiracy object.

It is not literally one giant book.

It is a distributed archive of:
- letters;
- bank records;
- photographs;
- military files;
- affairs;
- secret children;
- bribes;
- medical records;
- police informants;
- debts;
- crimes.

It began decades ago as mutual insurance between a small circle of elites.

Its principle:

> Nobody betrays the circle because the circle can destroy everybody.

Over time it became an unelected shadow institution.

Valère discovered enough of it to want publication.

## Why it is interesting for Plotbreak

The player can:

- destroy it;
- expose it;
- selectively leak it;
- steal it;
- use it;
- inherit it;
- sell pieces;
- blackmail people;
- return secrets to victims;
- create a more ethical intelligence network;
- ignore it.

No “correct” morality.

But consequences should be real.

**the Seven manipulated what happened after Valère’s death.**

# 26. CÉLESTE VARENNE

**Age:** 24  
**Role:** rival thief / social infiltrator / daughter of one of the Seven / possible romance / possible nemesis.

## Appearance

Striking young French woman.

- dark chestnut hair, usually worn in a soft bob or pinned elegantly for society events;
- grey-blue eyes;
- graceful athletic build;
- immaculate fashion;
- can move convincingly between evening gowns, riding clothes, servant disguise and practical burglary clothing.

She should look:
**dangerously expensive.**

## Public identity

Daughter of Henri Varenne.

Society knows her as:
- wealthy;
- sharp;
- difficult;
- unmarried;
- too interested in politics for some people’s comfort.

## Secret identity

A burglar/information thief using the alias:

**Nocturne**

Nocturne steals:
- letters;
- account books;
- blackmail material;
- occasionally jewelry to disguise the motive.

She is already investigating the Registry.

## Why

Céleste discovered her father was being blackmailed by people connected to Valère’s death.

She does not know the entire structure.

She wants evidence before anyone else can control it.

## Immediate want

Find Marcel Bellac’s stolen Registry material.

She learns someone opened his old Paris safe.

## Long-term want

Free her family from the Registry without simply replacing the people who control it.

## Fear

That her father is not merely compromised.

That he deserves what is coming.

## Secret

Céleste saw the player at the Beaumont gala.

More importantly:

she saw someone else enter the service corridor after them.

She stayed silent at trial because her father begged her to.

Her silence helped condemn the player.

This is an enormous relationship bomb.

Do not reveal it immediately.

## Values
- autonomy;
- intelligence;
- elegance under pressure;
- loyalty once earned;
- refusing humiliation.

## Boundary
She hates being owned, ordered around or treated as an accessory to her father.

## Voice

Quick.

Dry.

Socially precise.

Flirtation through challenge rather than endless compliments.

Example:

> “You break into my father’s office badly.”
>
> “I got in.”
>
> “Yes. That’s what makes the bad technique so upsetting.”

## Relationship space

Céleste can become:
- rival;
- co-conspirator;
- closest friend;
- lover;
- betrayed enemy;
- person who turns player in;
- person player exposes;
- person player kills;
- independent heroine who succeeds without them.

She must remain capable of saying no.

# 55. READY-TO-PLAY RESPONSE EXAMPLE — OPENING

Marcel has just placed the seven names on the floor.

### Suspicious

### Immediate
> I take the key first and the list second. “Explain while we move.”

### Dark
> I read the seven names once, fold the paper and put it inside my shirt. “Which one should be most afraid that I’m still alive?”

These are tone examples only.

Selecting one kills entire old choice set.

# 56. READY-TO-PLAY RESPONSE EXAMPLE — CÉLESTE ROBBERY

Céleste is holding the folder player came to steal.

### Charming
> I lower the pistol a few centimeters. “You know, I had an entire entrance planned. It’s rude to arrive first.”

### Threatening
> I lock the study door behind me. “Put the folder on the desk. Whatever game you’re playing with Varenne, tonight you lose.”

### Curious
> I glance at the folder, then at her gloves. “You’re not here for jewelry either. So tell me what you know before the guards remember they’re employed.”

All three create distinct stories.

# 57. READY-TO-PLAY RESPONSE EXAMPLE — RENAUD CORNERS PLAYER

# 58. READY-TO-PLAY RESPONSE EXAMPLE — CÉLESTE CONFESSION

Céleste admits she saw someone else in the corridor and stayed silent.

### Hurt
> I stare at her for a long time. “Eleven months. You let me spend eleven months in that place because your father asked you to stay quiet.”

### Controlled
> I look away before I say something I can’t take back. “Tell me everything you saw. We can decide what this does to us after.”

### Cruel
> I laugh once. There’s nothing amused in it. “And you’ve been standing beside me this whole time pretending we were hunting the same people.”

None prescribes forgiveness.

- victim;
- criminal;
- legend.

## SEVEN GRAVES — Dark/Rare
**Eligibility:** all or nearly all central Seven figures are dead primarily because of player’s revenge campaign.

The point is not “bad ending.”

Epilogue asks what revenge built afterward.

**Eligibility:** Céleste survives and player/Céleste become enduring partners in theft/investigation, romantic or platonic depending actual state.

**Eligibility:** earned romantic relationship with Céleste reaches stable future after resolving major guilt/trust conflicts.

# 95. WHY THIS SHOULD WORK FOR PLOTBREAK

This concept is unusually compatible with freeform AI storytelling because every major mechanic is semantic.

The player does not need a dedicated button to:

- lie;
- flirt;
- steal;
- disguise themselves;
- blackmail;
- forgive;
- duel;
- recruit;
- betray;
- publish;
- invent an alias.

They can simply SAY what they do.

That means natural-language freedom is not adjacent to the fantasy.

It IS the fantasy.

A static game would need:
- dialogue trees;
- disguise menus;
- quest scripting;
- fixed heist solutions.

Plotbreak can let the player invent them.
