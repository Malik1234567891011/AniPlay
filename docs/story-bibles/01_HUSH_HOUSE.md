# HUSH HOUSE

**Genre:** supernatural horror, mystery, romance, survival  
**One-line fantasy:** You rent a room in a beautiful old boarding house where the tenants are charming, the hallways move after midnight, and the most attractive girl in the building warns you that if you hear her knocking at 2:13 a.m., you must not open the door.

## Why this fantasy works

HUSH HOUSE should feel like being dropped into a prestige supernatural anime with a hot central girl, but the player owns the route. They can flirt, investigate, run, fight, ignore the mystery, move out, become obsessed with the house, destroy it, join whatever is inside, or turn the haunting into something else entirely.

The house is deeply authored. The player's route is not.

The horror must be understandable. The player should always know **what happened**, even when they do not know **why**.

## Story bible

Hush House is a six-story boarding house on Bellweather Street in the old quarter of **Morrowgate**, a modern coastal city of commuter rail, neon convenience stores, old brick blocks, rain-dark alleys, student bars, and century-old apartment buildings.

The player arrives because they need somewhere to live immediately. The reason is deliberately open: school, work, breakup, family issue, debt, travel, running away, or simply cheap rent. The landlady, **Mrs. Elara Vale**, offers Room 312 for a suspiciously low price. She is polite, warm, and refuses to discuss why the previous tenant left.

The central character is **Ayame Kurose**, 22, a striking tenant in Room 314. She is beautiful, stylish, dryly funny, sleep-deprived, and far less fearless than she pretends. On the player's first evening she gives them three rules as casually as someone explaining recycling:

1. If the hallway light outside Room 309 turns red, do not look through the peephole.
2. If the elevator opens onto a floor labeled **0**, do not step out.
3. If someone knocks at exactly **2:13 a.m.** and says they are Ayame, do not open it. Ayame promises she will never knock at that time.

The house does not operate like a puzzle with one sanctioned solution. Its supernatural behavior is responsive and personal. It learns routines, repeats voices, borrows memories, recreates objects, and produces “echoes” of people who have spent enough time inside.

Some phenomena are ghosts. Some are not.

The deepest authored truth is that Hush House was built over the sealed remains of **Saint Orra's Hospice**, which burned in 1911 during a city quarantine. The official death toll was falsified. Salvaged stone from the hospice was reused in the apartment foundation. Over decades the building developed a predatory form of memory. It does not simply haunt people. It **rehearses them**.

The house cannot leave, so it keeps versions of those who do. Strong emotional bonds make its copies more accurate because relationships give the building more behavioral information to imitate.

Ayame is tied to this personally. Her older sister **Mika Kurose** disappeared from Hush House four years ago. Police concluded she ran away. Ayame moved into the building to prove otherwise. She has never found Mika's body. She has seen Mika twice.

One central ambiguity should remain unresolved for a long time: when “Mika” appears, is she the original person, a trapped consciousness, a copy, an echo, or something else?

The house can be destroyed physically, but that does not automatically resolve what is beneath it. The player may free echoes, bargain with the building, expose the city's cover-up, leave with unanswered questions, become one of its permanent residents, or turn the supernatural rules to their advantage.

The story should alternate between ordinary life and horror. Breakfast with Ayame, late-night convenience-store runs, teasing, arguments about rent, neighbors complaining about noise, and awkward attraction are essential because horror matters more when normal life feels worth protecting.

## Tone and pacing

- Modern supernatural anime, not gore-first slasher.
- Sexy/attractive cast is fine, but avoid every scene reading like pin-up bait.
- Horror comes in memorable incidents rather than endless ominous prose.
- Quiet domestic scenes should be genuinely enjoyable.
- Use concrete sensory details: knocking, wrong voices, changed room layouts, familiar belongings in impossible places.
- Avoid lore dumping.
- One strange thing at a time.
- Mystery = player understands the event but not its cause.
- The player can author powers or absurd behavior if compatible; the world adapts.

## Player starting role

The player can define:
- why they moved to Morrowgate;
- work/studies;
- ordinary skills;
- personal history;
- whether they know occult material;
- compatible supernatural powers if explicitly established;
- style, personality, sexuality, habits, family.

### Minimal hard canon
- the player is newly associated with Hush House at story start;
- the house existed before them;
- Mika disappeared four years ago;
- the hospice fire happened;
- the house's memory phenomena are real.

Everything else should be open enough to adapt.

## Prologue

Rain has been falling over Morrowgate since noon.

By the time you reach Bellweather Street, the cheap apartment listing looks less like a bargain and more like a dare. Hush House rises between two newer buildings, six stories of dark brick, old bay windows, and warm yellow lights.

Room 312 is available immediately.

Mrs. Vale smiles when you ask why it is so cheap.

“Old building,” she says. “Old reputation.”

Behind her, the elevator dings.

The display reads **0** for half a second before becoming **3**.

Mrs. Vale does not look at it.

## Opening scene

The player has just finished dragging their last bag into Room 312. The apartment is unexpectedly nice: hardwood floors, tall windows, old radiator, compact kitchen, and a bedroom wall shared with Room 314.

A knock comes at 8:47 p.m.

Ayame Kurose is standing in the hallway in an oversized charcoal sweater, shorts, and house slippers, holding two canned coffees. She looks the player up and down, hands one over, and says:

“You're the new 312. Fine. Before you unpack anything expensive, there are three things you need to know.”

The player can laugh at her, flirt, refuse to listen, invite her in, walk away, ask about the house, or do something bizarre. The story must roll with it.

## Major cast

### Ayame Kurose
**Role:** neighbor, investigator, possible closest ally/romance, emotional center.  
**Appearance:** 22, long black hair with subtle wine-red undertone, amber-brown eyes, slim athletic build, stylish dark streetwear, fitted coats, oversized sweaters, boots.  
**Immediate want:** keep the new tenant alive long enough to determine whether Room 312 is “active” again.  
**Long-term want:** discover what happened to Mika.  
**Fear:** learning that the thing she spoke to really was Mika.  
**Secret:** once, at 2:13 a.m., Ayame opened the door. Something wearing Mika's face stood outside. She let it speak for almost ten minutes.  
**Voice:** dry, contemporary, teasing, blunt when afraid; short sentences under stress; hates melodramatic occult wording.  
**Possible evolution:** best friend, lover, suspicious rival, estranged ally, survivor who leaves, victim, antagonist if trust collapses.

### Mrs. Elara Vale
**Role:** landlady and keeper of institutional memory.  
**Want:** keep Hush House occupied, but not from cartoon villainy.  
**Secret:** her family has managed the property under an agreement they no longer fully understand.  
**Voice:** gracious, slightly old-fashioned, avoids direct lies by answering adjacent questions.

### Tomas Reed
**Role:** tenant in 206, paramedic, grounded skeptic.  
**Want:** finish night shifts, save money, move out with his younger brother.  
**Fear:** losing control of what he can rationalize.  
**Secret:** he has seen a dead patient in the stairwell twice.  
**Voice:** practical, dry, mildly exhausted.

### Nia Bell
**Role:** tenant in 405, art student/livestreamer.  
**Want:** turn the building's reputation into content.  
**Fear:** being irrelevant.  
**Secret:** she has footage of a hallway not on any floor plan.  
**Voice:** fast, online, curious, occasionally insensitive.

### “Mika”
**Role:** missing sister / echo / unresolved identity.  
**Rule:** never confirm too early what she truly is.  
**Voice:** warm and familiar with Ayame, but occasionally uses tiny details wrong.

## Behavioral state

Keep the number of variables small and mostly invisible.

### Ayame Trust
**Low:** guarded, shares only survival rules, avoids emotional vulnerability.  
**Medium:** investigates with player, admits fear, accepts help.  
**High:** shares Mika evidence, takes personal risks, can become deeply intimate.

### House Attention
**Low:** anomalies are subtle and impersonal.  
**Medium:** phenomena imitate voices, routines, and memories.  
**High:** the building actively stages scenes around the player and people they care about.

### Public Suspicion
Tracks whether police/neighbors/media see the player as credible, unstable, dangerous, or useful.

The player should experience these through behavior, not permanent numbers.

## Keyword / lore book

- **Hush House:** construction history, impossible renovation record, Vale ownership.
- **Room 309:** officially vacant; red-light phenomenon; previous tenant disappearance.
- **Floor 0:** no architectural equivalent; elevator anomaly.
- **2:13 a.m.:** time recorded in hospice fire-alarm log.
- **Saint Orra's Hospice:** quarantine facility destroyed in 1911.
- **Mika Kurose:** Ayame's missing older sister.
- **Mrs. Vale:** family property agreement.
- **The Echoes:** building-created rehearsals/copies.
- **Bellweather Street:** convenience store, tram, police precinct, bars.
- **Sub-basement:** sealed structure beneath the legal basement.

## Active story pressures

- Ayame is getting closer to something connected to Mika.
- Room 312 begins reproducing details from the player's life.
- Nia may publish footage that attracts attention.
- Mrs. Vale wants to prevent authorities from opening the sub-basement.
- The house gets better at imitating people as relationships deepen.
- A redevelopment inspection threatens to expose sealed parts of the property.

These are pressures, not quests.

## Emergent routes

Possible, never mandatory:
- investigate with Ayame;
- romance Ayame;
- distrust Ayame and ally with Mrs. Vale;
- expose Hush House publicly;
- move out and continue elsewhere in Morrowgate;
- deliberately provoke the house;
- communicate with “Mika”;
- destroy or seal the building;
- accept the house and become part of it;
- become an occult investigator;
- use self-authored supernatural powers;
- kill/betray major characters and continue with consequences.

## Ending space

### Dawn on Bellweather — Standard
Core danger is neutralized or escaped; player survives and chooses what comes next.

### Mika Comes Home — Rare
A version of Mika persists outside Hush House, with ambiguity intact.

### The House Forgets You — Rare
Player severs Hush House's memory of them and leaves.

### Room 312 — Hidden
Player willingly binds themselves to the house or becomes an echo.

### Ashes Don't Remember — Rare
The building and buried ward are dealt with in a way that genuinely ends the phenomenon.

### Ayame's Choice — Rare
Deep relationship with Ayame; final decision prioritizes a shared future over solving every mystery.

### The New Keeper — Hidden
Player takes over Mrs. Vale's role or creates a new bargain.

## Shared visual direction

Use the broad visual language visible in the OOC examples the user shared: polished high-detail anime/manhwa mobile key art, attractive expressive characters, crisp facial rendering, clean linework, soft painterly cel shading, dramatic cinematic lighting, strong color separation, glossy premium finish, readable silhouettes, and fashion-conscious costumes. Avoid photorealism, chibi proportions, muddy AI texture, generic same-face characters, and excessive particles.

For covers: vertical 2:3 to 9:16, one dominant character or tight 2–3 character ensemble, large readable faces, obvious genre cue in the background, negative space for a programmatic title overlay, and no generated typography.

For ordinary reaction art: preserve the exact same character reference, outfit continuity, age, facial structure, hair, eye color, and personality. Favor waist-up or three-quarter portraits with clear emotion. Reuse is acceptable.

For hero frames: wider cinematic composition that depicts the actual important event. These can be generated asynchronously and should never block the story.
