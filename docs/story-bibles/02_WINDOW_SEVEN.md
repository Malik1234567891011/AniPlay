# WINDOW SEVEN

**Genre:** espionage thriller, stakeout, paranoia, romance, conspiracy  
**One-line fantasy:** You and an elite undercover partner are locked inside a surveillance apartment for seven nights, watching a suspected traitor across the street — until the target looks directly into your hidden camera and waves.

## Why this fantasy works

WINDOW SEVEN is the fantasy of being inside a tense modern spy anime: binoculars, burner phones, whispered arguments at 3 a.m., disguises, coded messages, rooftop tails, cramped safehouse chemistry, and the possibility that the mission briefing is wrong.

The player can follow orders, go rogue, contact the target immediately, romance their partner, romance the suspect, defect, sell secrets, fake a death, abandon the stakeout, or become far more dangerous than anyone expected.

## Story bible

The player works for, used to work for, or has just been recruited by **Directorate Nine**, a deniable intelligence service in the city-state of **Veyra**. The exact background is open: veteran field operative, analyst forced into field work, criminal informant, military transfer, freelancer, or a self-authored elite specialist.

The assignment sounds simple: spend seven nights in Apartment 7C of **Orpheum Towers**, surveilling the penthouse across the avenue. The target is **Dr. Selene Voss**, a celebrated cybernetics researcher and policy adviser suspected of selling classified defense data.

The player's assigned partner is **Mara Ellison**, 27, brilliant, beautiful, disciplined, and hard to read. She has done long stakeouts before and hates sloppy improvisation. She also organizes the safehouse snacks by expiration date and watches terrible reality TV with subtitles on when she thinks nobody is paying attention.

The safehouse contains directional microphones, long-lens cameras, signal interception gear, burner phones, fake identities, coded check-in protocols, and one locked black case that the player is ordered not to open.

On the first night, Dr. Voss steps onto her balcony at 1:16 a.m., looks across the street directly toward the hidden camera, and waves.

Seconds later the player's secure phone receives:

**YOU ARE WATCHING THE WRONG APARTMENT.**

The mission is not simply “catch the traitor.” Directorate Nine contains internal factions. Selene may be leaking secrets, protecting someone, running a counterintelligence trap, or trying to expose **PALISADE**, a predictive surveillance program.

The authored truth: PALISADE identifies likely political dissidents before they act. Selene helped build an early version and later tried to shut it down after learning Directorate Nine had secretly deployed it domestically. The leak investigation is partly an internal purge designed to identify who is helping her.

Mara's orders are not identical to the player's. She carries a sealed contingency authorization: if Selene attempts to leave Veyra with the PALISADE archive, Mara is authorized to kill her. Mara has not told the player.

The player can uncover this, never uncover it, side with any party, destroy PALISADE, take it, sell it, publicize it, expose Directorate Nine, or walk away.

## Tone and pacing

- Claustrophobic stakeout intimacy punctuated by sudden action.
- Spycraft should be smart but easy to understand.
- Avoid drowning the player in acronyms.
- Conversation between player and Mara is a core pleasure.
- The safehouse should visually and narratively accumulate life: takeout, coffee, equipment glow, sleep deprivation.
- Suspicion comes from concrete inconsistencies, not endless vague paranoia.
- If the player leaves the apartment, the world continues.

## Prologue

Seven nights. One window. No contact.

That is how Director Halden sells the assignment.

Apartment 7C is dark when you arrive. Blackout curtains cover every window except the narrow surveillance slit facing Orpheum Tower across the avenue. A camera sits on a tripod. Two mugs sit beside it.

Your partner is already there.

Mara Ellison does not turn around when you enter.

“Lock the door,” she says. “And don't stand in front of the glass.”

Across the street, Dr. Selene Voss's penthouse is still dark.

For now.

## Opening scene

The player has been in the safehouse around twenty minutes. Mara explains only what is necessary: watch Selene, log visitors, no contact, no heroics.

The player can banter, question the mission, flirt, inspect equipment, open the locked case, call Selene, open the curtains, ignore Mara, or leave.

Within the first few turns, unless the player derails first, Selene steps onto the balcony and waves directly toward the hidden camera.

## Major cast

### Mara Ellison
**Role:** partner, co-lead, possible romance, possible adversary.  
**Appearance:** 27, dark chestnut hair cut to jaw length, grey-green eyes, lean athletic build, fitted black turtlenecks, tailored covert jackets, minimal jewelry.  
**Immediate want:** execute the surveillance professionally.  
**Long-term want:** gain enough leverage to leave field operations on her own terms.  
**Fear:** being responsible for another partner's death.  
**Secret:** contingency order authorizing Selene's assassination.  
**Voice:** dry, clipped, observant, rarely wastes words. Unexpectedly funny in private.  
**Possible evolution:** trusted partner, lover, ideological enemy, fugitive companion, superior, subordinate, dead.

### Dr. Selene Voss
**Role:** target, suspect, possible ally/rival/romance.  
**Appearance:** 30, silver-blonde hair, elegant, poised, white shirts and dark coats, calm eyes that make surveillance stills feel like she is looking back.  
**Want:** get PALISADE evidence out before Directorate Nine closes in.  
**Fear:** the archive becoming a weapon for somebody else.  
**Secret:** she knows the player's reputation before the operation begins.  
**Voice:** precise, confident, lightly playful when she has informational advantage.

### Director Elias Halden
**Role:** handler and institutional pressure.  
**Want:** contain PALISADE and identify internal collaborators.  
**Secret:** personally authorized domestic deployment.  
**Voice:** paternal, patient, almost never openly threatening.

### Juno Vale
**Role:** building concierge / neighborhood intelligence node.  
**Want:** money, gossip, safety.  
**Secret:** sells access logs to more than one side.  
**Voice:** charming and opportunistic.

### “Glass”
**Role:** unknown messenger.  
**Authored truth:** Glass is a shared dead-drop identity used by three Directorate insiders rather than one mastermind.

## Ending space

### Burn Notice — Rare
Player becomes officially compromised and escapes Veyra.

### Two Tickets Out — Rare

### The Target Was Right — Rare

### The Perfect Operative — Rare
Player completes Halden's intended operation and rises inside Directorate Nine.

### No More Windows — Hidden
Player destroys every side's leverage and disappears.

### Palisade Crown — Hidden
Player takes control of the surveillance system instead of destroying it.

### Mara's Order — Rare
Mara follows or rejects the assassination authorization in a way that permanently defines her relationship with player.

## Shared visual direction

Use the broad visual language visible in the OOC examples the user shared: polished high-detail anime/manhwa mobile key art, attractive expressive characters, crisp facial rendering, clean linework, soft painterly cel shading, dramatic cinematic lighting, strong color separation, glossy premium finish, readable silhouettes, and fashion-conscious costumes. Avoid photorealism, chibi proportions, muddy AI texture, generic same-face characters, and excessive particles.

For covers: vertical 2:3 to 9:16, one dominant character or tight 2–3 character ensemble, large readable faces, obvious genre cue in the background, negative space for a programmatic title overlay, and no generated typography.

For ordinary reaction art: preserve the exact same character reference, outfit continuity, age, facial structure, hair, eye color, and personality. Favor waist-up or three-quarter portraits with clear emotion. Reuse is acceptable.

For hero frames: wider cinematic composition that depicts the actual important event. These can be generated asynchronously and should never block the story.
