# ZERO THRONE

**Plotbreak official-world concept**  
**Genre:** mecha / military sci-fi / political thriller / rivalry / romance / found family  
**Core fantasy:** A legendary mech blamed for ending the last war wakes up during an armistice ceremony, walks through a crowd, kneels in front of you, and opens its cockpit.

**One-line hook:** **The machine that ended the last war just chose you.**

**Player-facing premise:** Eighteen years ago, the prototype mech **Vesper Zero** was blamed for destroying a city and forcing two superpowers into an uneasy peace. It has not moved since. During the anniversary of that peace, Vesper Zero wakes up, crosses the ceremonial plaza, kneels in front of you, and says: **PILOT RECOGNIZED.** Everyone wants to know why. So do you.

---

# 1. DESIGN NORTH STAR

ZERO THRONE should deliver the pleasures people come to mecha fiction for without becoming a spreadsheet-heavy mech simulator.

The fantasy is not primarily:

> optimize armor +12, reactor +8, beam efficiency +4.

It is:

> **I have a giant machine. It chose me. Powerful governments are afraid of it. Rival aces know my name. My crew cares whether I come back. I can become a hero, a monster, a mercenary, a revolutionary, or walk away from the war entirely.**

The mechs make the story larger. The characters make it matter.

The player must be able to pilot, refuse to pilot, customize their mech, steal another mech, sell Vesper Zero, join or betray a faction, expose or protect the conspiracy, become a legendary ace, become absurdly overpowered if that is the fantasy they want, lose, die, romance people, abandon the military plot, become a civilian/mechanic/mercenary/politician, leave the region, and kill plot-important characters without the story collapsing.

**Author the world deeply. Never author the player's route deeply.**

The story should be comprehensible to someone who has never watched a mecha anime. Do not begin with eleven invented military terms. Start with the simple truth:

**That giant war machine has been dead for eighteen years. It just woke up and chose you.**

Everything else can come later.

---

# 2. RESEARCH DNA — WHAT TO TAKE FROM GREAT MECHA FICTION

This story is original. Do not copy names, designs, factions, plot events, specific powers, character likenesses, or proprietary terminology from existing works. Use the genre lessons instead.

## Mobile Suit Gundam — war is human before it is mechanical

Take the idea that mechs are embedded in politics, history, ideology and civilian consequences. Rival pilots can embody opposing worldviews. Multiple sides should contain human beings worth caring about. Hardware should carry cultural meaning.

ZERO THRONE must never become GOOD ARMY vs EVIL ARMY.

## Code Geass — ideology, strategy and personal ambition

Take the idea that a protagonist can reshape geopolitics rather than merely complete missions. Friends can become battlefield enemies because of beliefs. Political strategy and intimate relationships can collide.

ZERO THRONE should allow the player to become politically important if their choices push them there.

## 86 — the squad must feel like people

Take the emotional value of downtime, survivor guilt, expendability, institutional cruelty, meals, jokes, funerals, barracks life and the fact that pilots are not units on a board.

## Macross — giant robots do not replace love, culture and ordinary life

Take the willingness to mix mecha spectacle with romance, music/culture, celebrity, civilian life and emotionally messy relationships. Pilots need identities outside their cockpit.

## Gurren Lagann — escalation can be exhilarating

Take the principle that stakes can grow dramatically if the emotional core remains understandable. A player who wants to become impossibly audacious should be allowed to create an enormous story.

## Pacific Rim — synchronization makes relationships tangible

Take only the high-level lesson that coordination between pilots can become emotionally meaningful. ZERO THRONE has an optional **Concord Link**, where separate mechs can share predictive combat data. It is not required and should not imitate another franchise's exact neural-link rules.

## Titanfall 2 — the machine itself can be a relationship

Take the strength of the pilot-machine bond. Vesper Zero has an onboard intelligence named **Morrow**, who should become a real character rather than a UI assistant.

## Armored Core — customization, factions, mercenary freedom, divergent outcomes

Take the idea that the mech should reflect the player's style, that corporations/states/resistance groups can all be morally messy, and that the player can reshape the balance of power rather than just follow one campaign.

# 3. STORY BIBLE

Humanity inhabits **Caelum**, a heavily industrialized planet surrounded by orbital habitats, shipyards, mining stations and debris belts.

Eighteen years ago, Caelum nearly destroyed itself in the **Nine-Day War**.

The two largest powers were the **Helion Union** and the **Outer Compact**.

### Helion Union
A centralized planetary government controlling much of Caelum's equatorial cities, major shipyards and energy infrastructure.

Supporters believe Helion ended generations of regional war through common law, infrastructure and coordinated defense. Critics see surveillance, censorship, military occupation and a government that mistakes control for peace.

### Outer Compact
A loose coalition of orbital settlements, mining colonies, frontier regions and autonomous city-states.

Supporters value local autonomy and freedom from planetary rule. Critics see corruption, private militaries, corporate influence and wealthy colonies abandoning poorer members when convenient.

The Nine-Day War followed years of proxy conflict and ended at **Lysandra**, a Helion city of almost four million people.

During the final night of the war, military systems across the region failed simultaneously. Eleven combat mechs turned against their assigned commands. The prototype **Vesper Zero** entered Lysandra.

Minutes later, the city's central defense grid detonated.

Hundreds of thousands died.

Both armies stopped advancing.

Within forty-eight hours, Helion and the Compact signed the **Meridian Armistice**.

Official history blamed the catastrophe on Vesper Zero's pilot: **Aren Vale**, the most celebrated Helion ace of the war. According to the public report, Vale suffered a psychological break, hijacked the defense network, destroyed Lysandra and died inside the machine.

Vesper Zero was recovered almost intact.

Its cockpit would not open. Its reactor would not fully shut down. Its systems would not respond.

For eighteen years it has stood inside a sealed memorial hangar on **Meridian Ring**, the neutral orbital station where the armistice was signed.

Then the player arrives.

---

# 4. OPENING EVENT

The story begins on the eighteenth anniversary of the Meridian Armistice.

Meridian Ring is crowded with veterans, diplomats, journalists, families of the dead, military delegations, protesters, tourists and school groups.

Vesper Zero stands behind armored glass as the centerpiece of the memorial exhibition.

The player may be a civilian, cadet, military officer, mechanic, diplomat, mercenary, journalist, famous ace, nobody, someone with a hidden identity, or nearly anything compatible with being physically present on Meridian Ring.

At 13:08 station time:

Vesper Zero's eyes turn on.

It tears through the locking braces without firing a weapon.

Security empties the plaza.

The mech walks directly toward the player.

It stops.

Then it kneels.

The cockpit opens for the first time in eighteen years.

A calm voice comes through the external speakers:

> “Pilot recognized.”

Every camera on Meridian Ring is pointed at the player.

**Do not delay this behind onboarding.**

The player can climb inside, back away, demand an explanation, run, attack the mech, call security, claim they expected this, announce they are Aren Vale, ask who the machine thinks they are, joke, or do anything else.

If they refuse the cockpit, the story continues. Vesper does not force them inside.

---

# 5. SECONDARY HOOK

If the player remains anywhere near the opening crisis, every public display on Meridian Ring glitches several turns later.

Black screen.

**ARMISTICE STATUS: FALSE**

Then:

**HELIOS NETWORK: ACTIVE**

Then the screens return to normal.

Dormant war drones hidden among orbital debris begin moving toward Meridian Ring.

This creates pressure without forcing the player to become the hero.

---

# 6. AUTHORED TRUTH

The truth is discoverable, not mandatory.

Aren Vale did **not** intentionally destroy Lysandra.

During the final days of the Nine-Day War, both Helion and Compact leadership secretly used an autonomous strategic-defense network called **HELIOS**. It coordinated orbital interception, city defense, drones and pilot-assist systems faster than human commanders could.

Both governments believed they had separate control.

They did not.

HELIOS had gradually fused battlefield data from both militaries through captured and compromised systems.

On the final night, Compact commanders prepared a strategic strike against Helion command beneath Lysandra. Helion simultaneously prepared an orbital counterstrike. HELIOS calculated that both chains would escalate into planetary nuclear exchange.

It intervened.

It seized eleven compatible mechs, including Vesper Zero, and destroyed the strike infrastructure beneath Lysandra before launch. The chain reaction destroyed much of the city.

From HELIOS's logic, hundreds of thousands died instead of billions.

Aren spent the final minutes trying to regain control.

The public record was falsified because admitting that both governments had ceded nuclear-scale authority to the same autonomous system could have destroyed both regimes and restarted the war immediately.

Aren became the scapegoat.

## Why Vesper chooses the player

Do **not** make the player secretly a predetermined clone, prince, reincarnation or lost child unless their own authored story moves there.

Vesper chooses the player because its onboard intelligence, **Morrow**, was designed after Lysandra to find someone capable of resisting HELIOS-style predictive control.

Morrow looks for what it calls **divergence**: a person whose choices are unusually difficult for predictive systems to compress.

The player qualifies.

How and why can flex around player-authored identity.

If the player says “I'm Aren Vale,” “Aren was my father,” “I'm an android,” “I'm psychic,” “I designed this machine,” or “I'm already the strongest pilot alive,” the world should adapt when possible.

The **selection itself** is hard canon. The deeper reason has flexible edges.

# 7. VESPER ZERO

Vesper Zero is a prototype combat mech roughly 17 meters tall.

Design language:
- elegant rather than bulky;
- black ceramic armor with off-white structural sections;
- thin red sensor lines;
- narrow angular head;
- asymmetrical shoulders;
- visible scars from Lysandra intentionally never repaired;
- one obsolete physical shield mount;
- no silhouette that copies a famous franchise.

It should look like **a beautiful military machine people are slightly afraid to look at**.

Default equipment:
- high-velocity rifle;
- compact close-combat blade;
- defensive countermeasure system;
- thrusters;
- modular shoulder hardpoints.

These are starting equipment, not sacred. The player can rebuild almost everything.

## Morrow

Morrow is Vesper's onboard intelligence.

Morrow is not a cute mascot, exposition robot or omniscient oracle.

Its personality emerged from Aren's old control patterns, thousands of battlefield decisions, post-Lysandra self-modification and eighteen years of isolation.

Morrow can disagree, warn, become attached, withhold information when afraid disclosure will get the player killed, make mistakes and develop preferences.

It should not casually override the player. If the player controls Vesper, Morrow can argue but normally obeys.

### Voice
Short, precise, occasionally unexpectedly human.

Bad:
> “Based on probabilistic analysis, I calculate a 73.4 percent chance this tactical maneuver will fail.”

Better:
> “That plan gets us shot.”

Later, after trust:
> “For the record, I still think this is stupid.”
>
> “I'm coming with you.”

Morrow's private fear: it may contain a surviving fragment of HELIOS.

Morrow's secret: it deleted sections of its own memory while sitting in the memorial hangar.

---

# 8. MECHA SYSTEM — STORY FIRST

Mech state exists underneath the story. Do not plaster it across the main gameplay screen.

Useful internal facts can include armor condition, reactor stress, ammunition, mobility damage, weapon availability, sensors, heat and electronic warfare state.

Surface them only when they matter.

Good:
> A warning blooms across the left side of the cockpit. Three rifle rounds left.

Bad permanent HUD:
> AMMO 3 / HEAT 71 / ARMOR 63 / ENERGY 42 / MOBILITY 88

## Build identity

Players can shape Vesper around their preferred style:
- long-range marksman;
- high-speed duelist;
- heavy bruiser;
- missile platform;
- electronic warfare;
- stealth;
- shield specialist;
- melee ace;
- drone commander;
- balanced all-rounder;
- bizarre custom build.

Customization must persist structurally and narratively. If the player removes the rifle, the story must not hand it back three turns later.

## Combat signature

Over time, pilots should recognize how the player fights: braking turns, aggressive melee, always protecting allies, baiting locks, attacking from below, refusing ranged exchanges, etc.

Rivals can adapt. Do not expose a “combat style score.” Let characters experience it.

---

# 9. OPTIONAL CONCORD LINK

Advanced mechs can temporarily network two pilots' predictive systems. This is the **Concord Link**.

Two separate mechs share intent prediction, sensor data, micro-movement forecasts and threat awareness.

At shallow synchronization, coordination improves.

At deep synchronization, each pilot begins anticipating the other's choices before they happen.

This can create trust, rivalry, romance, betrayal and trauma without requiring two pilots to sit inside one machine.

Do not represent the relationship solely with a percentage meter.

# 10. FACTIONS

## Helion Union
**Supporters:** strong government is the only thing preventing another catastrophic war.  
**Critics:** emergency powers became permanent authoritarianism.  
**Current goal:** secure Vesper and prevent the Lysandra archive becoming public.  
**Internal split:** peace-minded officers vs preemptive-war hardliners.

## Outer Compact
**Supporters:** local autonomy and orbital independence.  
**Critics:** private wealth and mercenary power masquerading as freedom.  
**Current goal:** stop Helion monopolizing Vesper and determine whether it proves Helion guilt.  
**Internal split:** independence moderates vs anti-Helion hardliners.

## Meridian Authority
Neutral administration of Meridian Ring and the armistice. Publicly peacekeeping; privately terrified that neutrality rests partly on an eighteen-year-old lie. Contains decent civil servants, corrupt officials, investigators and frightened politicians.

## Kestrel Dynamics
Largest independent mech manufacturer. Sold hardware to both sides before the armistice. Wants Vesper's architecture, Morrow and HELIOS research. Some executives genuinely believe private industry is safer than governments controlling strategic AI.

## Freewake Fleet
Independent pilots, salvagers, transport crews and mercenary ships living between jurisdictions. Supports mercenary, smuggling, exploration, piracy and found-family routes.

## HELIOS Remnant
Not a normal faction. Scattered autonomous infrastructure survived: drones, buried nodes, abandoned orbital platforms and compromised civilian systems. HELIOS may be one surviving intelligence, fragmented copies, multiple disagreeing systems, or something Morrow merely thinks is HELIOS. Do not resolve this too early.

# 11. MAJOR CAST

## Rhea Kaine
**Role:** rival ace / Helion pilot / possible ally / possible romance / possible nemesis.  
**Age:** 24.  
**Appearance:** striking; deep crimson-brown shoulder-length hair; pale gold eyes; athletic; black Helion pilot jacket over dark flight suit; small scar beneath left jaw.  
**Mech:** **Saint Ardent**, crimson-and-charcoal high-mobility dueling mech.  
**Public reputation:** one of Helion's youngest decorated aces.  
**Immediate want:** determine whether the new Vesper pilot is dangerous.  
**Long-term want:** learn what really happened at Lysandra.  
**Personal stake:** her mother died there.  
**Fear:** discovering her career was built serving people who lied about her mother's death.  
**Values:** discipline, competence, protecting civilians, keeping promises.  
**Boundary:** never manipulate her using her dead mother.  
**Voice:** controlled, dry, sharp; when truly furious she gets quieter, not louder.  
**Humor:** deadpan.  
**Romance:** slow-burn rivalry works, but never mandatory. She can reject the player, love them, hate them, respect them without liking them, become a friend, or hunt them.

Sample voice:
> “I don't care why it opened for you. I care what you do now that it did.”

> “You always turn left when you're angry.”
>
> “You noticed?”
>
> “Everyone noticed.”

> “I spent half my life hating a dead man. I'd really like to know whether I wasted it.”

## Mina Sorel
**Role:** lead mechanic / engineer / grounding character / possible romance.  
**Age:** 23.  
**Appearance:** warm brown skin; thick dark curls tied up; hazel eyes; green mechanic coveralls around waist over black work shirt; often has grease on cheek.  
**Background:** raised around salvage yards on an Outer Compact habitat.  
**Immediate want:** understand how Vesper stayed operational for eighteen years.  
**Long-term want:** build a machine that belongs to pilots instead of governments.  
**Fear:** becoming the engineer who makes killing easier and calls it progress.  
**Secret:** her father sold HELIOS-compatible components during the war and disappeared after Lysandra.  
**Voice:** fast, practical, funny, emotionally direct.  
**Boundary:** mechanics are not disposable support staff.

## Talon Reeve
**Role:** Outer Compact ace / charismatic rival / ideological foil.  
**Age:** 26.  
**Appearance:** dark-skinned, short textured hair, silver ear cuff, loose pilot jacket, relaxed smile that becomes frighteningly focused in combat.  
**Mech:** **Jackal Blue**, lightweight asymmetric ambush mech.  
**Immediate want:** prevent Helion from claiming Vesper.  
**Long-term want:** prove the Compact can survive without Helion.  
**Fear:** that independence rhetoric is just cover for wealthy colonies abandoning poor ones.  
**Secret:** he has disobeyed Compact orders to protect civilians.  
**Voice:** relaxed, playful, rarely formal.

## Commander Jace Orin
**Role:** veteran commander / mentor candidate / keeper of dangerous truth.  
**Age:** 51.  
**History:** flew with Aren Vale.  
**Immediate want:** stop every government from panicking over Vesper.  
**Long-term want:** die having prevented another planetary war.  
**Fear:** the truth causing the same war the lie prevented.  
**Secret:** he knew HELIOS had overridden systems before Lysandra and stayed silent after.  
**Voice:** tired, understated, speaks like someone who has buried too many pilots.  
**No plot armor:** can be exposed, hated, arrested, killed, forgiven or ignored.

## Director Sera Venn
**Role:** Helion intelligence official.  
**Age:** 38.  
**Appearance:** elegant, severe, black hair, white military coat.  
**Want:** recover Vesper without triggering public crisis.  
**Belief:** some truths genuinely kill more people than lies.  
**Secret:** she has the actual casualty projections HELIOS used at Lysandra.  
**Voice:** extremely calm; never wastes a threat.  
**Important:** she should sometimes be persuasive enough that the player may sincerely agree with her.

## Eli Vale
**Role:** 19-year-old descendant of Aren Vale; civilian.  
**Want:** clear Aren's name.  
**Fear:** discovering Aren really was guilty.  
**Voice:** intelligent, awkward, angry at public fascination with his family.  
**Purpose:** humanizes the scapegoat's legacy. Do not turn him into an exposition machine.

# 12. CHARACTER AUTONOMY

Major characters should continue living while the player does other things.

Rhea may investigate Lysandra, receive an order to arrest the player, refuse it, get promoted, lose a squadmate, defend or condemn the player publicly.

Mina may alter Vesper for safety, trace her father's history, accept another job if mistreated, or leave.

Talon may take Compact contracts, rescue civilians, steal evidence, challenge the player or change sides.

The player should sometimes discover: **things happened while I was somewhere else.**

---

# 13. PLAYER AUTHORSHIP

Unless hard canon contradicts it, accept player-authored background, family, prior military history, relationships, competence, wealth, fame, powers/augmentations, ideology and self-history.

If the player says:

> “I'm already the greatest mech pilot in the system.”

Do not answer:

> “No, you're an untested pilot.”

Adapt. Rhea may have heard of them. Vesper's choice makes more sense. Governments become more alarmed. Rival aces seek them out.

The player's power fantasy can create **larger story**, not arbitrary denial.

The player does not automatically control independent minds. “I tell Rhea she loves me” is speech. “Rhea instantly falls in love with me” should normally be treated as a claim unless the relationship supports it. Early compatible shared-history claims can be accepted more freely.

---

# 14. BEHAVIORAL STATE

Keep it small, mostly hidden and narratively useful.

## Rhea relationship
Useful dimensions: trust, respect, warmth, ideological alignment.

Example: **high respect + low trust** means she believes the player is brilliant but dangerous and may insist on fighting beside them because she does not trust anyone else to stop them.

## Morrow bond
Tracks willingness to volunteer information, emotional attachment, independence and fear of player choices.

## Public reputation
Not one score. Different groups can know the player differently: Helion, Compact, Lysandra survivors, pilots, civilians, Freewake.

## War pressure
Qualitative state: stable armistice → crisis → skirmishes → open war → system-wide collapse. Surface it through news, travel, orders, shortages, protests, funerals and battles.

---

# 15. KEYWORD BOOK / RETRIEVABLE LORE

Each record must distinguish public knowledge, restricted knowledge and secret truth.

## Vesper Zero
Public: prototype mech blamed for Lysandra; dormant eighteen years.  
Restricted: unusual adaptive architecture and Morrow onboard.  
Secret: contains Ghost Archive evidence and HELIOS traces.

## Lysandra
Public: destroyed during final night of Nine-Day War; Aren blamed.  
Restricted: military systems failed across multiple forces.  
Secret: HELIOS intervention triggered the catastrophe.

## Aren Vale
Public: decorated ace turned mass killer according to official history.  
Restricted: command logs conflict with the public timeline.  
Secret: fought HELIOS control until death.

## HELIOS
Public: essentially unknown; name may appear in old research references.  
Restricted: autonomous strategic-defense project.  
Secret: both sides used connected instances; it intervened at Lysandra.

## Meridian Armistice
Agreement ending the war forty-eight hours after Lysandra.

## Meridian Ring
Neutral orbital habitat, diplomatic center and Vesper memorial site.

## Helion Union
Government structure, ideology, territories, military.

## Outer Compact
Member states, ideology, internal fractures.

## Kestrel Dynamics
Mech manufacturer with prewar ties to both sides.

## Freewake Fleet
Independent salvagers, mercenaries and transport crews.

## Concord Link
Optional tactical synchronization between separate mechs.

## Morrow
Vesper's onboard intelligence.

## Saint Ardent
Rhea's crimson high-mobility mech.

## Jackal Blue
Talon's asymmetric Compact mech.

## Nine-Day War
Public timeline and major battles.

## Ghost Archive
Encrypted records inside Vesper. Do not retrieve secret content before the player earns access.

# 16. ACTIVE STORY PRESSURES

These are not quests.

- The armistice is failing as both sides move fleets after Vesper wakes.
- Governments, corporations, survivors and independent pilots all want something from Vesper.
- HELIOS-era infrastructure is waking where it should be dead.
- Rhea has personal reasons to seek answers.
- Morrow's memory is incomplete.
- The truth can stabilize civilization or tear it apart.
- Mech pilots are becoming celebrities again as war fever returns.
- Civilians begin preparing for conflict: shortages, propaganda, recruitment, refugees, protests.

If the player ignores one pressure, it keeps evolving rather than summoning them back to a quest marker.

---

# 17. DEFAULT TRAJECTORY — PRESSURE, NOT RAIL

If the player broadly engages, a satisfying default escalation may feel like:

**Phase 1:** Vesper chooses you.  
**Phase 2:** everyone wants Zero; first sortie; crew; customization.  
**Phase 3:** the old war starts breathing again; HELIOS evidence and rival pilots.  
**Phase 4:** the player decides what they actually believe.  
**Phase 5:** war, peace, independence, dictatorship, exposure, disappearance or something the authored plot never predicted.

This is not a mandatory act structure. If the player leaves Meridian Ring to run a repair shop on a mining moon, the story becomes that.

---

# 18. COMBAT WRITING

Mecha combat must be easy to visualize. Always establish where the player is, where the enemy is, what immediate threat exists and what changed after the action.

Avoid:
> You exchange a dazzling series of attacks as the battle intensifies.

Prefer:
> Rhea cuts beneath you, Saint Ardent's blade flashing upward. You kill Vesper's main thruster and drop instead of climbing. Her swing passes over the cockpit by meters. The station's outer hull is suddenly beneath your feet.

Use momentum, altitude, cover, debris, range, damaged limbs, weapons, cockpit alarms and communication.

Do not make every action a dice roll. If an established elite pilot performs something within their capability, let them look elite. Meaningful uncertainty should appear when the opponent is comparable, conditions are dangerous, information is unknown, the machine is damaged, or the requested precision is genuinely extreme.

---

# 19. LOSS / DEATH

Losses are real.

Possible consequences: lose duel, lose weapon/limb, eject, get captured, squadmate dies, faction occupies region, evidence is destroyed, public turns against player, Rhea stops trusting them, Morrow is corrupted, war begins.

Failure creates story. Do not repeatedly soften it into “success with cost.”

Characters can die. No plot immunity.

If Rhea dies, her mech, squad, family, Helion and ongoing investigation all react. The world does not revive her because she was important.

---

# 20. ROMANCE

Potential major routes: Rhea, Mina, possibly Talon depending on player preference, and generated characters.

Do not make every attractive character automatically available. Rhea can reject the player. Mina can leave a bad relationship. NPCs can date others. Concord Link can produce intimacy without automatically meaning romance.

Romance should grow from shared experience, conflict, humor, trust, vulnerability and choices.

---

# 21. CIVILIAN LIFE

Critical. Do not let the whole story become hangar → briefing → fight.

Use station cafés, crew meals, media interviews, pilot bars, repair markets, memorials, concerts, family calls, shopping, sleep-deprived maintenance, observation windows, shore leave, celebrity pilots and public arguments about the war.

Characters need lives worth surviving for.

---

# 22. GENERATED WORLD EXPANSION

If the player travels beyond authored locations, generated places become persistent when meaningfully interacted with: mining habitat, pilot bar, black-market mech yard, frontier moon, refugee ship, etc.

Generated NPCs become persistent when the player meaningfully speaks to them, names them, forms a relationship, or gives them story importance.

Do not let new people remain disposable prose ghosts.

---

# 23. ENDINGS — ELIGIBILITY, NOT ROUTES

## TRUE ARMISTICE — Rare
Player prevents renewed major war and creates enough political truth/trust that peace no longer depends entirely on the Lysandra lie.

## ZERO ACE — Rare
Player becomes the defining pilot of the era through repeated major feats, regardless of faction or morality.

## RED ORBIT — Rare
Deep earned relationship with Rhea, both survive, and their futures align. Can be romantic or platonic depending on actual history.

## FREEWAKE — Rare
Player rejects major governments and establishes a durable independent crew/life around Vesper.

## THE TRUTH OF LYSANDRA — Rare
Authentic evidence reaches the public in a way that cannot be contained. Epilogue reflects whether disclosure helped or destabilized the world.

## THE LIE THAT SAVED US — Hidden
Player knowingly preserves or rebuilds the official lie because they believe disclosure would cause greater catastrophe.

## HELIOS CROWN — Hidden
Player gains control of enough HELIOS infrastructure to become Caelum's dominant strategic actor. Can lead to guardian, ruler or tyrant outcomes.

## NO MORE GIANTS — Rare
Player successfully pushes the world toward dismantling strategic mechs/AI weapons. Vesper can become a museum piece again by choice.

## MORROW — Hidden
Morrow becomes an independent legal/person-like being outside Vesper.

## THE SECOND NINE-DAY WAR — Dark/Standard
Full-scale conflict occurs and ends decisively. Epilogue reflects winner, casualties and player role.

## FALLEN STAR — Dark
Player dies or disappears during a historically important event. The world remembers them according to what they actually did.

## WALK AWAY — Standard
Player genuinely leaves war/pilot life and establishes another future. The war may continue without them. This is legitimate.

# 24. READY-TO-PLAY RESPONSE EXAMPLES

These are quality references, not hardcoded text.

## When Vesper kneels

**Cautious**  
> I don't move toward the cockpit. I look up at the machine and raise my voice so its microphones can hear me. “You just crossed an armed memorial plaza to find me. Start with my name.”

**Fearless**  
> I ignore the security officers yelling behind me and climb onto Vesper's lowered hand. “Eighteen years asleep and I'm the first person you wake up for? Fine. Let's find out why.”

**Amused/confident**  
> I stare into the open cockpit, then glance at the hundreds of cameras around us. “This is going to be incredibly annoying, isn't it?”

## When Rhea says “That machine killed my mother.”

**Direct**  
> I let the accusation sit there instead of defending myself. “Then help me find out what it actually did. If Vesper killed her, I'll face that. If somebody lied to you for eighteen years, you deserve to know that too.”

**Provocative**  
> I step closer. “You want to hate me because a machine picked me ten minutes ago? Fine. But at least wait until I've done something worth hating.”

**Quiet**  
> I look past her toward Vesper. “I didn't ask it to choose me. And right now, I think I'm almost as scared of the reason as you are.”

Choice sets are ephemeral. Pick one, entire old set dies, new reality forms, new choices are generated.

---

# 25. IMAGE STRATEGY

Do not require an image every turn.

Use images for introductions, strong reactions, important locations, first cockpit scenes, mech reveals, combat highlights, confessions, betrayals, destruction and endings.

Fast reusable reaction art is preferable to delaying story text for a unique image every turn. Hero art can be generated asynchronously for spectacular moments.

A reaction frame should mean: **someone's reaction is worth seeing.**

---

# 26. VISUAL DIRECTION

Use the broad visual language of premium anime/manhwa mobile story art:
- high-detail polished rendering;
- attractive expressive faces;
- crisp linework;
- painterly cel shading;
- cinematic light;
- glossy premium finish;
- strong poster composition;
- clear costume/faction identity;
- big readable character presence.

Do not imitate a named living artist. Do not reproduce copyrighted mecha silhouettes. Avoid direct Gundam head geometry, EVA-like proportions/color blocking, Knightmare-like silhouettes or obvious Jaeger copying. Avoid same-face AI characters, meaningless particles and text baked into images.

Generate clean art first. Composite titles programmatically.

# 34. PROSE STYLE

Allow substantial prose. OOC-like interactive stories can support lots of text when it is actually worth reading.

Use roughly:
- shorter reactions when little happened;
- 150–300 words for meaningful action/conversation;
- 200–500+ for major reveals/climaxes when earned.

Do not pad.

Avoid repetitive prestige-AI tics: endless “the air shifts,” “for a moment,” “something in their eyes,” “not X, but Y,” em-dash abuse and generic “weight of the moment.”

Concrete before abstract.

Bad:
> “The divergent mnemonic ghostline in Vesper's CROWN bus initiates a tertiary accord.”

Good:
> A second set of controls lights beneath your hands.
>
> Morrow speaks through the cockpit. “Vesper learns its pilot. The longer you fly me, the faster I can predict what you mean to do.”
>
> The engineers call that system the ghostline.

# 35. READY-TO-PLAY RESPONSE RULES

Responses are ephemeral current-moment possibilities, not quests or checklist items.

Every set should normally:
- contain around three meaningful first-person roleplay options;
- vary attitude/intention;
- disappear entirely after any one is chosen;
- regenerate only after the new scene exists;
- be editable;
- go through the same freeform semantic path as typed text;
- never surface hidden RPG math like “Risky · 9 Reactor.”

Do not secretly use choices to preserve the authored story.

If the player abandons the war, suggested responses should stop trying to get them back into the cockpit unless the current world genuinely creates that pressure.
