# PRIMAL CROWN

**Genre:** prehistoric fantasy adventure, factions, beast riding, war, exploration  
**One-line fantasy:** In an age before kingdoms, every great people rides the beasts of the old world — raptors, mammoths, sabertooths, horned giants, and sky-lizards — and the player can rise from an unknown rider to reshape the entire age.

## Why this fantasy works

PRIMAL CROWN should feel like a giant, colorful prehistoric anime adventure rather than a history lesson. Humans coexist with dinosaurs and Ice Age megafauna, and cultures have grown around the animals they bond with.

The player can choose a faction, reject every faction, tame an unexpected beast, become a trader, warlord, scout, peacemaker, outlaw, beast hunter, explorer, shaman, or legendary rider.

The world is authored deeply. The player's route is not.

## Story bible

The story takes place in **The Wild Age**, thousands of years before the first stone kingdoms in a fictional world where dinosaurs and Ice Age megafauna coexist across enormous ecological zones.

Human societies are tribal/confederate but not written as stupid “cavemen.” They have oral law, sophisticated trade routes, medicine, astronomy, metalworking in rare regions, ritual, engineering adapted around huge animals, and complex diplomacy.

The defining culture/technology is **bond riding**. Certain animals can be raised, trained, or ritually paired with humans. Different peoples specialize in different species and riding philosophies.

There is no single universal magic system. The world contains rare “old blood” phenomena: unusual beasts with impossible traits, ancestral caves that affect animal behavior, volcanic glass that resonates with certain creatures, shamans who understand patterns others do not, and ancient bones associated with strange events. Players can author more supernatural abilities if compatible.

The central geopolitical pressure is the **Great Migration**, a once-in-a-generation movement of enormous herds toward the southern grasslands. Whoever controls the migration corridors controls food, trade, breeding stock, and survival for years.

At the same time, a legendary albino tyrannosaur called **White Maw** has begun attacking bonded animals without feeding on them. Some think it is diseased. Some think it is sacred. Some think another faction is driving it.

The player begins at **Sunscar Crossing**, a neutral seasonal market where five major factions meet before the migration.

The player can be from any faction, mixed heritage, factionless, outsider, captive, trader, hunter, messenger, or entirely self-authored.

The authored truth: a chain of volcanic tremors has altered migration routes. White Maw is not inherently supernatural by default; it has been repeatedly wounded and displaced by a hidden network called the **Ashen Hand**, which uses fire, scent, and captured predators to destabilize rivals and control chokepoints.

The player may never discover this. The world must still work.

## Major factions

### Emberclaw
Fast grassland riders bonded mainly with raptors and light carnivores.  
**Culture:** speed, daring, scouting, personal reputation.  
**Political weakness:** clans argue constantly.  
**Misconception:** outsiders treat every Emberclaw rider as a raider.

### Stoneback Confederacy
River-valley farming/trade communities using triceratops, ankylosaurs, and giant herbivores for defense and transport.  
**Culture:** oath law, engineering, trade, long-term planning.  
**Strength:** richest infrastructure in the region.  
**Weakness:** bureaucracy and elite families.

### Frostfang Tribes
Northern hunters who ride sabertooths, dire wolves, woolly rhinos, and mammoths.  
**Culture:** kinship, survival competence, quiet hospitality.  
**Misconception:** outsiders mistake reserve for hostility.

### Skyfire Nomads
Cliff and desert peoples using pterosaurs and giant avian mounts.  
**Culture:** messengers, scouts, long-distance traders, aerie settlements.  
**Strength:** information and speed.  
**Weakness:** vulnerable ground holdings.

### Mireborn
Wetland federation using hadrosaurs, crocodilian river beasts, giant turtles, and amphibious mounts.  
**Culture:** waterways, medicine, fishing, plant knowledge.  
**Political position:** underestimated by other factions.

### Ashen Hand
Hidden network rather than a normal start faction.  
**Methods:** predator manipulation, sabotage, arson, captured beasts, fear.  
**Goal:** collapse old alliances and control migration chokepoints.

## Tone and pacing

- Big adventurous anime energy.
- Wonder matters as much as danger.
- Animals behave like animals with personality, not motorcycles.
- Factions must be nuanced rather than good tribe/bad tribe.
- Political conflict should remain legible.
- Avoid fake caveman speech.
- Quiet campfire scenes, grooming mounts, family meals, market bargaining, and rituals build attachment.
- Huge battles should be rare enough to feel huge.

## Prologue

The earth moves before the herds do.

At dawn, cups rattle on the trade tables of Sunscar Crossing. Far beyond the tents, thousands of long-necked grazers lift their heads at once.

Then the horizon begins to move.

The Great Migration has started early.

Every faction at the crossing understands the same thing:

someone is about to lose a homeland.

## Opening scene

The player is at Sunscar Crossing during the opening migration festival.

Around them:
- Emberclaw raptor riders race through dust;
- Stoneback merchants unload cargo from horned giants;
- Frostfang hunters lead sabertooths through crowded lanes;
- Skyfire riders circle overhead;
- Mireborn traders arrive by river.

A panicked juvenile predator breaks loose near a crowded market lane.

This is not a mandatory tutorial fight. It is an immediate situation the player can:
- calm;
- tame;
- kill;
- ignore;
- exploit;
- help someone escape;
- chase for fun;
- walk away from.

Soon after, unless the player derails first, news arrives that White Maw struck a bonded herd north of the crossing.

## Beast-bond principles

Bonding is not a class lock.

The player can:
- start with a traditional faction mount;
- bond a different species;
- have no mount;
- lose/change mounts;
- attempt absurd bonds;
- raise juveniles;
- become known for unusual animals.

Mounts have temperament and memory. They can fear, refuse, become injured, bond with other people, or die.

## Behavioral state

Keep state small and story-serving.

### Beast Bond
Qualitative trust between player and important mount(s). Influences handling, willingness, and risk.

### Faction Standing
Prefer faction-specific qualitative standing rather than one global “reputation” score.

### Migration Stability
World pressure tracking how badly food routes, alliances, and herds are destabilizing.

Mostly invisible; experienced through access, reactions, and events.

## Shared visual direction

Use the broad visual language visible in the OOC examples the user shared: polished high-detail anime/manhwa mobile key art, attractive expressive characters, crisp facial rendering, clean linework, soft painterly cel shading, dramatic cinematic lighting, strong color separation, glossy premium finish, readable silhouettes, and fashion-conscious costumes. Avoid photorealism, chibi proportions, muddy AI texture, generic same-face characters, and excessive particles.

For covers: vertical 2:3 to 9:16, one dominant character or tight 2–3 character ensemble, large readable faces, obvious genre cue in the background, negative space for a programmatic title overlay, and no generated typography.

For ordinary reaction art: preserve the exact same character reference, outfit continuity, age, facial structure, hair, eye color, and personality. Favor waist-up or three-quarter portraits with clear emotion. Reuse is acceptable.

For hero frames: wider cinematic composition that depicts the actual important event. These can be generated asynchronously and should never block the story.
